## 2. Why "Compact City" Now? The Reason Toyama City Commands Nationwide Attention

The core of Toyama City's Compact City policy is to maintain efficient administrative services and uphold residents' living standards despite population decline and an aging demographic. By preventing urban sprawl and encouraging population concentration in designated residence promotion zones, the city can provide high-quality public services while curbing infrastructure maintenance and renewal costs.

A particularly noteworthy trend for 2026 is the revision of Toyama City's "City Center and Public Transport Corridor Residence Promotion Project" (まちなか・公共交通沿線居住推進事業). Starting this April, the policy shifted significantly from supporting new housing construction to subsidizing the acquisition and utilization of "used housing (existing stock)." As a result, generous subsidies are now available to households that purchase and renovate eligible pre-owned homes in central areas. This measure, which prevents the increase of vacant homes and effectively circulates existing housing stock, is also highly praised from a Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) perspective.

According to data obtained by this site, Mekiki Research (物件目利きリサーチ), the area around Toyama Station is designated as a "Commercial Zone," permitting intensive land use with a building coverage ratio of 80% and a floor area ratio of 500%. This combination of deregulation and subsidy systems acts as a powerful driving force, attracting people and capital to Toyama City's urban core.

## 3. A Life Connected by LRT: Comfortable Transport Convenience Without a Car

When discussing Toyama City's Compact City, one cannot overlook the LRT (Light Rail Transit) system that runs north-south and covers the entire city. In regional cities that were once the epitome of car-centric societies, there is no other example of such a successful return to public transportation.

Toyama Station, served by conventional lines like the Ainokaze Toyama Railway and the Hokuriku Shinkansen (北陸新幹線), is a massive transport hub handling 31,492 passengers daily. The LRT runs directly under the elevated tracks of Toyama Station, seamlessly connecting the north and south exits and dramatically improving mobility within the city.

For the elderly, the low-floor LRT vehicles—which have no steps and are easy to board—act almost like moving walkways. Having a transport network that allows safe, weather-independent access to major city facilities even after giving up a driver's license strongly supports the independent living of seniors. This overwhelming transport convenience is undoubtedly the biggest factor underpinning the solid real estate demand in central Toyama City and along the LRT corridors.

## 5. A Practical Buyer's Perspective: Toyama City's Appeal for Prospective Homeowners

For those considering buying a home for their own use, Toyama City offers a major advantage through its robust subsidy programs. By utilizing the aforementioned "City Center and Public Transport Corridor Residence Promotion Project," buyers can receive partial grants for the acquisition and renovation costs of pre-owned condos and houses in the city center, making it possible to significantly reduce initial expenses.

On the other hand, one aspect that must never be ignored when purchasing real estate is disaster risk. According to hazard data from the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (国土地理院) and other sources obtained for this report, while there is no "landslide" risk in central Toyama City, there is a very high risk for "flooding," rated at a maximum depth rank of 5 (estimated inundation of 10 to 20 meters). Given the geographical characteristics of hosting multiple rivers including the Jinzu River (神通川), preparing for flood risks is essential. When choosing a property, we strongly urge buyers to take measures such as selecting higher floors in condominiums or elevating foundations for detached houses, and to always check the latest hazard maps before purchasing.

Additionally, educational environments require careful attention. In our public transaction data, school district information for specific elementary and junior high schools was listed as "null (unassigned/missing data)." Because school districts in central urban areas are often reorganized, it is absolutely vital for families buying property to individually verify the designated school zones with the Toyama City Board of Education (富山市教育委員会) using the latest information.
