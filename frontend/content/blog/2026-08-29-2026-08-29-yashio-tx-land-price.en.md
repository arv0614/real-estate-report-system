## 1. Introduction: The Opening of the Tsukuba Express and the Transformation of Yashio City

The opening of the Tsukuba Express in 2005 brought a paradigm shift to the real estate market of Yashio City, located in the southeastern part of Saitama Prefecture. Previously lacking a railway station and reliant on buses and private cars, the birth of Yashio Station drastically reduced travel time to central Tokyo.

More than 20 years have passed since its opening, and up to 2026, Yashio City has continuously promoted systematic urban planning. The area around the station, in particular, has completely transformed from its once-cluttered scenery into a modern urban space where well-maintained road networks, lush green parks, and highly convenient commercial facilities harmonize seamlessly.

According to the data extracted this time via Mekiki Research, the sample size of real estate transactions in the target period from 2021 to 2025 reached 1,503. This abundant transaction volume proves that Yashio City is not just experiencing a temporary boom, but has matured into a market consistently chosen by homebuyers and real estate investors alike.

## 5. Expanding Housing Demand Centered on Families and Shifts in the Target Demographic

Along with the improvement of urban functions, a clear shift can be seen in the target demographic flowing into Yashio City. Once primarily composed of locals with existing ties to the area or those working in the industrial sector, the main driver of housing demand is now first-time homebuyers (families) in their late 20s to 40s who commute to central Tokyo.

The zoning environment indicated by the data—"Category I Residential Zone, Building Coverage Ratio 60.0%, Floor Area Ratio 300.0%"—means a very high degree of freedom from a real estate development perspective. In areas where a 300% floor area ratio is permitted, the development of mid-to-high-rise condominiums for families or rental residences becomes highly profitable, leading to aggressive land acquisitions by developers.

As mentioned earlier, the price range of an average transaction price of approximately 40.43 million yen and a median of 30.00 million yen is a "sweet spot" where power couples and families with a household income of around 6 to 8 million yen can comfortably secure a mortgage. This depth of real demand forms the resilience of Yashio City's real estate market against economic fluctuations. There are also cases such as a wooden house (95 sqm, built in 2013) in the Oaza Tsurugasone (大字鶴ケ曽根) area being traded for 6.50 million yen. It can be inferred that this was influenced by the building's physical depreciation and individual factors, suggesting that "diamonds in the rough" for renovation investments are still lying dormant in the market.

## 6. Land Price Trends and Area-Specific Price Movements in Yashio City Over the Past 10 Years

To anticipate the latest land price forecasts for 2026, an area-specific price movement analysis based on past actual transaction data is indispensable. In this dataset, the minimum transaction price was 430,000 yen, while the maximum reached a staggering 1.2 billion yen. This overwhelming price gap vividly illustrates the diversity of real estate usage (residential, commercial, large-scale logistics facilities, etc.) within Yashio City.

Particularly noteworthy is a 1,200 sqm land transaction confirmed in the Oaza Tsurugasone area. The transaction price was 70.00 million yen, which translates to a unit price of approximately 58,000 yen/sqm. However, the urban planning designation for this property is an "Urbanization Control Area" (市街化調整区域). Because the construction of new buildings is restricted in principle within Urbanization Control Areas, there is a strong downward pressure on land prices compared to Urbanization Promotion Areas (市街化区域) such as Category I Residential Zones.

Thus, even within Yashio City, there is a world of difference in land price trends and investment returns between "land-readjusted Urbanization Promotion Areas within walking distance of the station" and "Urbanization Control Areas far from the station." The average unit price of about 160,000 yen/sqm is merely a flattened overall figure. When investing in real estate, the ability to sharply discern—"Mekiki" (目利き)—the micro-level zoning and urban planning designations is crucial.

## 7. Analyzing Future Investment Potential and Risks with "Mekiki Research"

While we have explained the high potential of Yashio City thus far, what is equally or even more important than returns in real estate investment is "risk management." When considering investing or purchasing a home for personal use in Yashio City, assessing flood risk is absolutely unavoidable.

According to the hazard data extracted for this article, while the phenomenon of landslide disasters at the target locations is rated as "No Risk," flood risk is clearly assessed as "At Risk." What requires even more caution is that its maximum inundation depth rank is at an extremely high level of "4 (5 to 10 meters)." Yashio City is situated in a low-lying area surrounded by Class A rivers such as the Nakagawa River (中川) and the Ayase River (綾瀬川). Historically, concerns over inland flooding and river swelling during typhoons and heavy rains have been prevalent.

A maximum inundation risk of 5 to 10 meters means that the second to third floors of typical buildings could be submerged. Therefore, when purchasing or developing properties in this area, the following countermeasures are essential:
1. Adding comprehensive water damage coverage to fire insurance
2. For detached houses, securing high foundation levels and installing major equipment (such as distribution boards and water heaters) on upper floors
3. For condominium investments, evaluating designs where the first floor is used as a pilotis (e.g., parking space) and residential units are placed on the second floor or higher

To enjoy the investment potential (high yields and expectations for asset value appreciation), investors must possess the calm analytical skills to correctly recognize these hazard risks and price in insurance premiums and physical countermeasure costs into their yield calculations beforehand.
