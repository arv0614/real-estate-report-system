Okazaki City in Aichi Prefecture, the birthplace of Tokugawa Ieyasu, is a city steeped in history and culture. Today, this city is on the verge of a major transformation, driven by the "QURUWA Strategy," an ambitious urban renewal project with a vision for 2030. This public-private partnership, which goes beyond mere infrastructure development to fuse historical assets with modern urban functions, is set to make a significant impact on the real estate market.

In this article, from the perspective of a real estate analyst, we will focus on the area around Higashi-Okazaki Station, the core of the QURUWA Strategy. Based on the latest real estate transaction and neighborhood data obtained by Mekiki Research on August 2, 2026, we will thoroughly analyze and explain its investment potential and future prospects. We will delve into the core reasons why Okazaki City is such a noteworthy market for assessing future asset value.

## 1. Why is Okazaki City in the Spotlight? A Complete Overview of the "QURUWA Strategy" Public-Private Partnership

The "QURUWA Strategy" promoted by Okazaki City is a long-term urban vision aimed at regenerating the area within a roughly one-kilometer radius of Okazaki Castle into an attractive space where history, culture, and people's lives are integrated. The name is derived from *sōgamae*, the outer defensive fortifications that once protected both the castle and the surrounding town. In the same spirit, the strategy seeks to create new value in line with modern lifestyles while leveraging historical assets.

A key feature of this strategy is its foundation in public-private partnerships (PPP), involving private businesses and citizens, rather than a top-down, government-led development approach. This is expected to foster sustainable and creative urban development.

Specifically, the strategy is composed of the following three pillars:

1.  Public-Private Partnership Projects in the Otogawa Riverfront Area: Transforming the riverside space into a hub for relaxation and activity.
2.  Development of the Higashi-Okazaki Station Area: Enhancing its function as a transportation hub and creating new landmarks.
3.  Historical Urban Development: Preserving and utilizing historical assets such as Okazaki Castle and the Old Tokaido Road to boost their appeal as tourist resources.

Through the synergy of these projects, Okazaki City has the potential to evolve beyond being a mere bedroom community or industrial city into a city with its own unique brand value. The clear target year of 2030 also provides real estate investors with a crucial guideline for forecasting market growth from a medium- to long-term perspective.

## 2. The Project's Core: Progress and Impact of the Higashi-Okazaki Station Area Development

The most critical piece in determining the success of the QURUWA Strategy is the development of the area around Higashi-Okazaki Station, the city's main gateway. As a major station on the Meitetsu Railway, Higashi-Okazaki Station is used by many people daily as a key access point to Nagoya.

The ongoing development plan includes elevating the station building, extending the pedestrian deck that connects the north and south sides of the station, and attracting commercial facilities and hotels. These initiatives are expected to dramatically improve not only transportation convenience but also the area's appeal as a commercial hub.

According to Mekiki Research data, the area around Higashi-Okazaki Station is designated as a "Commercial Zone," with a building coverage ratio of 80% and a floor-area ratio of 400%. This indicates high development potential, with conditions suitable for high-rise condominiums and large-scale commercial buildings, creating fertile ground for redevelopment projects. It is easy to imagine that the birth of a tower condominium directly connected to the station or a new landmark commercial complex would significantly boost the real estate value of the entire area.

Improved convenience also influences people's lifestyles. As the area becomes a more compact, station-centered city, a lifestyle less dependent on automobiles will become possible. This will act as a catalyst to attract new residents, such as young people who prefer urban living and seniors seeking convenience in their retirement.

## 3. The Transformation of the Otogawa Riverfront and the Creation of New Vibrancy

In parallel with the development of Higashi-Okazaki Station, the revitalization of the Otogawa Riverfront area is also underway. The riverbed, which once primarily served flood control functions, is now being reborn as a "profitable park."

Through public-private partnerships, a succession of new attractions are being created, including open-air cafes, the introduction of activities like canoeing, and the regular hosting of markets and music events. As a result, the riverside is shifting its role from a place to simply pass through to a "destination" where people gather, stay, and spend money.

This enhancement of the riverfront's appeal has a direct positive impact on the value of surrounding real estate. In particular, demand for condominiums with river views overlooking the Otogawa and for detached houses that enjoy the tranquil riverside environment is expected to grow further. A quality living environment is one of the most powerful factors in attracting families. According to Mekiki Research data, the school district for this area includes Umezono Elementary School and Kozan Junior High School. The combination of this educational environment and the rich natural setting of the riverfront makes it highly likely that the area will be recognized as extremely attractive to households with children.

## 5. Risks for Investors to Consider: Hazard Information and Zoning

On the other hand, risk assessment is essential for any real estate investment. The risk of natural disasters, in particular, can significantly affect asset value. According to Mekiki Research's hazard information, there are two points of caution in the Higashi-Okazaki Station area.

First is the flood risk. The data indicates a potential maximum inundation depth of 10 to 20 meters, which corresponds to Rank 5, the highest level on a five-point danger scale. Given its proximity to the Otogawa River, this is an unavoidable risk. When selecting a property, it is crucial to check the detailed hazard maps published by the local government to confirm the projected flood zones. It is also necessary to consider whether the building has been elevated or waterproofed and whether appropriate flood insurance is available.

Second is the landslide risk. The data points to a danger of "debris flows". When considering areas slightly away from the city center and closer to hilly terrain, a detailed investigation by experts is required to determine if the location is designated as a landslide warning area and if the safety of the developed land is secured.

Furthermore, from a micro-perspective, confirming the "zoning" is also important. The sample data shows various regulations depending on the area, including "Semi-Industrial Zone," "Category 1 Medium-to-High-Rise Exclusive Residential Zone," and "Category 1 Residential Zone." For example, in a "Semi-Industrial Zone," small factories can be built in addition to residences, which means the surrounding environment could change. Aligning your investment strategy (e.g., family-oriented rentals vs. single-person studios) with the land's potential (floor-area ratio and building coverage ratio) is a key factor that will determine the success of your investment.

## 7. Okazaki's Unique Appeal and Investment Edge: A Comparison with Toyota and Kariya

When discussing the Mikawa region of Aichi Prefecture, a comparison with Toyota City and Kariya City, two of Japan's leading corporate castle towns, is essential. While these cities boast strong economic foundations and high income levels as global hubs for the automotive industry, they also have an industrial structure that is heavily dependent on specific corporations.

In contrast, Okazaki City's strength lies in its "diversity." It offers a balanced coexistence of historical and cultural assets as the birthplace of Tokugawa Ieyasu, a rich natural environment provided by the Otogawa River, and robust commercial functions. This is a unique charm that Toyota and Kariya do not possess. The "QURUWA Strategy" aims to further polish this diversity and enhance the city's brand value.

From an investment perspective, this diversity also leads to risk diversification. It allows for stable real estate demand that is less susceptible to the trends of a single industry. The trend of "separation of workplace and residence," where people working in Toyota or Kariya choose to live in Okazaki, may accelerate in the future. The ability to offer a lifestyle that combines the conveniences of an industrial city with the richness of history and nature on weekends is arguably the greatest investment appeal of the Okazaki real estate market.
