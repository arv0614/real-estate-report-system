In an era where regional revitalization has long been a key theme, many local cities across Japan are facing the common challenges of population decline and the hollowing out of their urban centers. Amidst this backdrop, cities that are leveraging their unique charm and potential to embark on bold urban development for the future are gaining attention. One such city is Tottori, a core urban center in the San'in region. Currently, a large-scale redevelopment project, the "Tottori Station Area Redevelopment Basic Plan," is underway around Tottori Station, the city's main gateway. This plan, with a vision for 2030, is poised to have a significant impact on the real estate market.

In this article, we will provide a thorough analysis of the entire redevelopment plan from the perspective of a veteran real estate analyst. How will this project, a cornerstone of the compact city strategy in an age of population decline, change the value of real estate in Tottori? Using the latest transaction data (as of August 6, 2026) acquired by Mekiki Research as evidence, we will forecast its future from multiple angles. We hope this report serves as a reliable aid in the decision-making process for those considering real estate investment or home purchase in the Tottori area.

## 1. Why Tottori, a Key Player in the San'in Region, Deserves Attention Now

When people hear of Tottori Prefecture, many might think of the Tottori Sand Dunes or its rich natural landscapes. However, from a real estate market perspective, Tottori City holds unique potential as an economic and cultural hub in the San'in region. In particular, the area around Tottori Station, the city's center, is the very heart of Tottori, with a concentration of commercial facilities, administrative bodies, and medical institutions.

A common challenge for regional cities in recent years has been the decline in vitality of central urban areas due to population outflow to the suburbs. In response, many municipalities have adopted the "compact city" model as a solution. This strategy aims to achieve both a reduction in administrative costs and an improvement in quality of life by concentrating urban functions in the city center and creating walkable communities.

The "Tottori Station Area Redevelopment Basic Plan" promoted by Tottori City is a tangible embodiment of this compact city strategy and could become a model case for other regional cities across Japan. The redevelopment of infrastructure and the construction of new hub facilities have the potential to change the flow of people, alter land use, and significantly impact real estate values. That is why, as real estate analysts, we cannot take our eyes off the developments in Tottori right now.

## 2. Population Decline and the Challenges of the Central Urban Area in Tottori

To understand the context of the redevelopment, it is first necessary to have an objective grasp of the current situation Tottori City faces. Like the rest of the nation, Tottori is grappling with the major structural challenges of long-term population decline and an aging society with a low birthrate. As a result, problems such as an increase in vacant stores and homes, and a decline in community vitality, are becoming apparent in the city center.

However, even under these circumstances, the real estate market is not completely stagnant. Let's look at the transaction data from 2021 to 2025 acquired by Mekiki Research for the Tottori Station area. During this period, 2,650 transaction samples were observed, indicating that a certain level of market liquidity is being maintained.

Turning to transaction prices, while the average transaction price is approximately 15.55 million yen, the median price is 11.00 million yen. This suggests that a few high-value transactions are pulling up the average, and it can be inferred that typical transactions are centered around the 10 million yen mark. In fact, while the highest transaction amount reached 420 million yen, there was also a case as low as 100 yen, showing the diversity of the market. Such data highlights the current reality that even in the city center, values are becoming increasingly polarized depending on location and property characteristics. The redevelopment plan is expected to be a catalyst to boost the value of these central properties and revitalize the market as a whole.

## 3. A Game-Changer? The 'Tottori Station Area Redevelopment Basic Plan'

The "Tottori Station Area Redevelopment Basic Plan" was formulated to overcome these challenges and pass on a sustainable city to the next generation. This plan goes beyond simple building reconstruction or road widening. With "Creating a Walkable City" and "Developing a Hub for Intergenerational Exchange" as its two main concepts, it aims to regenerate the area within an approximately 1 km radius of Tottori Station into an attractive space where everyone can spend their time comfortably.

The core of the plan consists of the following three pillars:

1.  Strengthening Transportation Hub Functions:
    *   Reorganizing the station plaza for seamless coordination between buses, taxis, and private cars.
    *   Building a safe and comfortable pedestrian network by extending pedestrian decks and ensuring accessibility.
    *   Improving connectivity with wider-area transportation to enhance its hub function as the gateway to the San'in region.

2.  Developing Hubs to Create Vibrancy and Exchange:
    *   Relocating and rebuilding the aging civic hall, and developing a multi-purpose complex that integrates a library, childcare support facilities, and more.
    *   Attracting new commercial facilities and offices by utilizing idle and underused land around the station.
    *   Creating event plazas that leverage history and culture, and improving the streetscape along main roads.

3.  Promoting Urban Living:
    *   Promoting the supply of high-quality urban-style condominiums targeted at young people and families with children.
    *   Supporting the renovation of existing housing stock (used condos and vacant homes).
    *   Creating a comfortable living environment by enhancing daily life amenities (supermarkets, medical institutions, educational facilities).

Through the synergistic coordination of these measures, the station area will transform from a mere transit point into a "destination" where people gather, live, and enjoy themselves. This directly enhances "convenience" and "attractiveness," the fundamental sources of real estate value, and the impact on the market is expected to be immense.

## 4. Project Analysis: Walkability and the Development of City Center Hubs

Among the plan's components, "walkability" and the "development of hub facilities" are expected to have the most significant impact on real estate values. In a regional city where society has been built around cars, creating a "town that's enjoyable to walk in" can transform lifestyles and stimulate new demand.

According to Mekiki Research data, the Tottori Station area is designated as a "Neighborhood Commercial Zone" for its land use, with a building coverage ratio of 80% and a floor-area ratio of 200%. This means the area has the potential for relatively flexible construction of commercial facilities, offices, and mid-to-high-rise condominiums. As the redevelopment improves pedestrian spaces and increases foot traffic, the latent potential of this land will be maximized. For example, it will become easier to construct condominiums with cafes or shops on the ground floor and well-designed commercial buildings, which is expected to enhance the charm and asset value of the entire streetscape.

Furthermore, the surrounding environment that supports quality of life is also crucial. The data indicates that this area belongs to the school district for Hisamatsu Elementary School and Minami Junior High School, making it an attractive location for families with children. Additionally, 15 medical institutions, including major hospitals like Ueda Hospital (Medical Corporation Ryokukai) and the Tottori Red Cross Hospital, are concentrated within a close radius, providing a secure living environment for the elderly as well. The addition of new cultural and childcare support facilities to these existing strengths will create an ideal compact city living environment where work, home, school, and leisure are all in close proximity. This is a critical factor that directly contributes to stable rental demand and the maintenance or improvement of the resale value of existing properties.

## 5. The Impact of Strengthening the Transportation Hub

Tottori Station, where the JR San'in Main Line and the Imbi Line converge, is a major railway station in the San'in region and the cornerstone of Tottori's transportation network. Strengthening its function as a transportation hub under the redevelopment plan is a crucial project that will heavily influence real estate values in the surrounding area.

The current station plaza is a mix of bus stops, taxi stands, and private car drop-off points, with a complex pedestrian flow. The plan aims to functionally separate and rearrange these transportation modes to create a space that is easy for everyone to understand and use. Particularly in the San'in region, which experiences frequent inclement weather, the development of covered pedestrian decks and arcades that allow access from the station to major facilities without getting wet will dramatically improve the city's comfort.

This will enhance convenience for business users, potentially stimulating demand for office space around the station. For tourists, smoother transfers to secondary transport (buses and taxis) will increase the area's appeal as a base for city sightseeing. This, in turn, will lead to improved profitability for hotels and commercial facilities, making the area more attractive for real estate investment.

While Mekiki Research has not yet acquired detailed data on passenger volume for Tottori Station, it is widely known that improvements in transportation infrastructure generally have a positive impact on surrounding land prices through increased convenience. As the plan materializes and the station becomes more convenient, a "concentric rise in prices" can be expected, with the benefits being greatest for properties closer to the station.

## 7. An Investment Perspective: Potential and Risks of the Tottori Station Area

When considering real estate investment in the Tottori Station area, it is essential to calmly evaluate both its potential and its risks.

[Potential]

1.  Capital Gains from Redevelopment: As the plan progresses, the asset value of the land itself may increase. Significant appreciation can be expected, especially in areas where zoning changes or increases in floor-area ratios are implemented.
2.  Stable Rental Demand from Compact City Development: As the population concentrates in the city center, rental demand around the station is expected to remain firm. Compact units for singles, DINKs (Dual Income, No Kids), and elderly households, in particular, could become a source of stable income gains.
3.  Relatively Affordable Prices: Compared to the Tokyo metropolitan area and other major regional cities, real estate prices in Tottori City are still at an affordable level. The ability to start investing with a smaller amount of capital is a major attraction.

[Risks]

1.  Presence of Hazard Risks: Understanding natural disaster risk is indispensable for real estate investment. According to Mekiki Research data, the Tottori Station area has a potential flood inundation depth of 3 to 5 meters (Rank 3) and is also flagged for sediment-related disaster risks such as "debris flows" and "landslides on steep slopes." When acquiring a property, it is crucial to check the specific risk level of the land on the detailed hazard maps published by the municipality and to take adequate measures, such as securing flood coverage in addition to fire and earthquake insurance.
2.  The Macro Trend of Population Decline: Redevelopment is ultimately a restructuring of the urban framework and cannot completely reverse the city's overall trend of population decline. In the long term, risks such as rising vacancy rates and falling rents should always be kept in mind.
3.  Risk of Project Delays or Changes: Delays and changes are common in large-scale redevelopment projects. It is necessary to consider the possibility that the plan could be scaled back or its completion date pushed back depending on changes in the socio-economic climate.

Understanding these risks thoroughly and selecting prime locations with low hazards from a long-term perspective will be the key to successful real estate investment in the Tottori Station area.
