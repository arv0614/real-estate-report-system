As we pass Respect for the Aged Day (敬老の日) on September 24, 2026, many families have likely taken the opportunity to discuss their parents' future living arrangements or their own retirement housing plans. In an aging Japan, there is a rapidly growing interest in transitioning from spacious suburban detached houses to highly convenient, compact homes in city centers—a trend often referred to as "downtown living" (まちなか居住).

Particularly noteworthy is Gifu City, Gifu Prefecture (岐阜県岐阜市), the focus of this article. Large-scale redevelopment is currently underway from the areas surrounding JR Gifu Station (JR岐阜駅) and Meitetsu Gifu Station (名鉄岐阜駅) toward the Yanagase area (柳ヶ瀬エリア). Gifu City is drawing significant attention as a prime model of a "compact city" (コンパクトシティ), where medical, commercial, and public infrastructures are seamlessly centralized within walking distance.

In this article, speaking from the perspective of a professional real estate analyst, we will unpack the latest real estate transaction data and redevelopment trends in Gifu City. We will thoroughly explore the asset value and future livability of downtown Gifu—highlighting why it is a compelling market not only for seniors seeking end-user homes but also for families raising children and savvy investors.

The central area stretching from Gifu Station to the Yanagase shopping district is currently undergoing a once-in-a-century transformation. Starting with the completion of the ultra-high-rise mixed-use building "Yanagase Grassle 35" in 2023, massive redevelopment projects are unfolding one after another.

A particularly noteworthy upcoming event is the fate of "ect", the commercial facility directly connected to Meitetsu Gifu Station. Although demolition was initially considered, plans were revised, and it is now slated to reopen as a brand-new commercial facility in FY2027. This will once again enrich the everyday shopping environment.

Furthermore, a massive twin-tower redevelopment is progressing at the north exit of JR Gifu Station. While the scale and schedule were adjusted due to recent spikes in construction costs, the project is moving steadily forward, aiming for the completion of the East Tower (approx. 30 stories) in FY2029 and the West Tower (approx. 20 stories) in FY2030. These new complexes will be seamlessly connected by pedestrian decks, drastically improving walkability around the station. From a real estate investment perspective, "now" (2026-2028)—before these core facilities are completed and the city's true value is fully priced into the market—is arguably the optimal time to consider purchasing, whether for personal use or investment.

## 6. Not Just for Seniors! Work-Life Proximity and Secure Environments for Families with Children

| Housing Support Systems and Hazard Info | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| Gifu City City Center New Home Acquisition Subsidy | Up to 10% of mortgage loan amount (base 200,000 - 500,000 yen)<br>*Families with children receive an additional 200,000 - 400,000 yen depending on the number of children (max 900,000 yen) |
| Flood Hazard Risk | At risk (Max inundation depth rank 4: 5-10m) *Caution required |
| Landslide Hazard Risk | No risk |

Seniors are not the only ones reaping the benefits of downtown living. In today's era where dual-income households are the norm, "work-life proximity" and "shopping convenience" provide invaluable time and breathing room for families raising children.

To encourage permanent settlement in the downtown area, Gifu City has established the "City Center New Home Acquisition Subsidy." Those relocating from within or outside the city can receive a base subsidy of up to 500,000 yen. Furthermore, families with children receive an additional 200,000 to 400,000 yen depending on the number of children, allowing for a generous maximum subsidy of 900,000 yen. Such strong governmental incentives serve as a massive tailwind for moving downtown.

However, one thing you must absolutely verify when purchasing real estate is "hazard risk." As the data shows, low-lying areas around Gifu Station, which are influenced by the Nagara River (長良川) and other waterways, carry a "Flood Hazard Risk (Max inundation depth rank 4: 5-10m)." On the other hand, there is no risk of landslides. When buying, it is crucial to take professional precautions: opt for middle-to-high floors in flood-resilient condominiums, or if buying a detached house, ensure the foundation is designed high and verify evacuation routes in advance. Properly recognizing risks and preparing accordingly is the ironclad rule of professional real estate selection.
