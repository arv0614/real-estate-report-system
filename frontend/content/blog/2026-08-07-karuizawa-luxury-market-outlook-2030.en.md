Karuizawa (軽井沢), Japan's premier luxury summer resort, is at a historic turning point. The widespread adoption of remote work, triggered by the COVID-19 pandemic, has profoundly shaken people's lifestyles and values, transforming Karuizawa from a mere "vacation home destination" to a "primary place of residence." Its ideal location—just about an hour from the Tokyo metropolitan area by Shinkansen—continues to attract those seeking to balance a professional career with a life immersed in a rich natural environment.

Furthermore, the rapid recovery of inbound tourism, fueled by the weak yen, is stimulating the luxury market for high-net-worth individuals and accelerating new development projects. Will this momentum continue toward 2030? Or will the overheated market enter a correction phase?

In this article, we will conduct a thorough analysis of the current state of land prices, the factors driving demand, and the potential risks, based on the latest real estate transaction data for the Karuizawa Station area obtained by the real estate analysis service Mekiki Research (物件目利きリサーチ). Using objective data as our compass, we will unravel the outlook for Karuizawa real estate investment leading up to 2030 from the perspective of a veteran analyst.

## 1. Introduction: Why is Karuizawa's Real Estate Market in the Spotlight Now?

Long beloved by political and business leaders as well as cultural figures, Karuizawa possesses a unique brand value cultivated through its history and prestige. However, its recent surge in popularity cannot be explained by tradition and prestige alone. The key to understanding today's Karuizawa market lies in three mega-trends.

First is the "diversification of work styles." As remote work and "workations" have become socially ingrained, a lifestyle untethered from a physical commute to the city has become a reality. This has dramatically increased Karuizawa's value, elevating it from a weekend getaway to a primary or secondary base for everyday life.

Second is the "stable demand from the wealthy." Against a backdrop of global monetary easing and a strong stock market, high-net-worth individuals from both Japan and abroad have been actively investing in real estate as part of their asset portfolios, particularly in resort properties with high scarcity value. Karuizawa's brand and abundant nature continue to make it an extremely attractive investment target for them.

Third is the "revival and qualitative shift in inbound tourism." As inbound demand shifts from simple sightseeing to "experiential consumption" that emphasizes long-term stays and unique experiences, Karuizawa is gaining international acclaim as a destination to enjoy Japan's nature and culture. This is driving the development of luxury hotels and condominiums, invigorating the entire real estate market.

The complex interplay of these factors has created an unprecedented boom in Karuizawa's real estate market. In this report, we will examine how these trends are reflected in actual real estate prices, using concrete data.

Investors must not be misled by a single figure like the average price; it is crucial to accurately grasp the market rate for their target property segment by referencing the median price and individual case studies.

## 3. Inbound Recovery and Affluent Demand: The Forefront of Luxury Villa and Hotel Development

When discussing Karuizawa's real estate market, the trends among inbound tourists, especially high-net-worth individuals, cannot be ignored. With the weak yen providing a tailwind, international travelers are rapidly returning, and Karuizawa is increasingly being chosen as their destination. To meet this demand, there has been a succession of new entries by foreign luxury hotel brands and developments of high-end villas that emphasize privacy.

These developments have a direct impact on surrounding land prices. For example, when a new luxury hotel opens, the brand value of the surrounding area increases, and demand for housing for employees is created. Furthermore, investment properties like hotel condominiums become attractive targets for both domestic and international investors, tending to trade at high prices.

The highest transaction price of ¥2.5 billion in the aforementioned data confirms that such large-scale deals by wealthy individuals and developers are a reality. Transactions of this magnitude serve as market benchmarks and exert a significant influence on the formation of land prices in the vicinity.

From an investor's perspective, this trend can be viewed in two ways. One is as a forward-looking investment in land for hotel or commercial facility development, anticipating future inbound demand. The other is to invest in residential areas nearby, aiming to capitalize on the ripple effect from development. Areas undergoing development as luxury resorts also see an improvement in the quality of the living environment, making them promising for long-term asset value appreciation. However, it is essential to thoroughly research development plans and local ordinances and to be cautious of overpaying based on excessive expectations.

## 4. Evolution as a Workation Hub: The Impact of Shinkansen Commuting and Corporate Relocation

Behind Karuizawa's solidifying status as a "workation sanctuary" is its outstanding accessibility. Karuizawa Station, operated by JR East, is just about an hour from Tokyo Station via the Hokuriku Shinkansen. This convenience has fundamentally changed Karuizawa's character, transforming it from a mere resort town into a "residential area that is an extension of the Tokyo metropolitan圏."

According to Mekiki Research data, the average daily number of passengers embarking and disembarking at Karuizawa Station is 5,436. This figure reflects the reality that the station is used daily not only by tourists but also by people commuting to the Tokyo area for work or school and those living a dual-residence lifestyle. An increasing number of companies are subsidizing Shinkansen commuter passes, making the lifestyle of living in Karuizawa while working at an office in the city no longer out of the ordinary.

This shift has also brought about a change in the nature of real estate demand. While compact villas mainly for weekend use were traditionally popular, there is now a growing demand for larger, more livable properties with high-speed internet and dedicated workspaces. Properties with vast lots exceeding 1,000 m², such as the 1,700 m² land-and-building example in Oaza Oiwake, are perfectly positioned to meet the needs of this new demographic.

Furthermore, it's not just individuals; corporate moves into Karuizawa are also on the rise. Companies are choosing Karuizawa as a strategic base to establish satellite offices or training facilities, aiming to enhance employee well-being and productivity. This corporate demand has a positive effect on the rental market and contributes to the stabilization of income gains for real estate investors.

## 5. Redevelopment Potential Analysis: The Karuizawa Kazakoshi Park Area

In addition to a macro perspective, micro-level area analysis is indispensable for investment decisions. Here, we explore the potential of the "Karuizawa Kazakoshi Park" area, which has been gaining significant attention in recent years. With the opening of the international educational institution "Karuizawa Kazakoshi Gakuen," this area is drawing keen interest from child-rearing families considering a move for educational purposes.

The public school district for the surveyed area includes Karuizawa Tobu Elementary School and Karuizawa Junior High School. The increase in families relocating in search of a high-quality educational environment is a powerful factor supporting demand for both rental and sales properties. The formation of a community centered around education has the potential to enhance the entire area's brand value over the long term.

The quality of the living environment is protected by Karuizawa's uniquely strict urban planning regulations. The transaction data shows that this area includes many zones designated as "Category 1 Low-Rise Exclusive Residential Zones," where building height and use are severely restricted. For example, in the Oaza Oiwake case, a building coverage ratio of 30% and a floor-area ratio of 50% apply to some lots. These are crucial rules for maintaining the classic Karuizawa landscape of lush, low-rise homes on spacious lots.

While these regulations limit development freedom, they play an extremely important role in preventing environmental degradation from disorderly development and in preserving the scarcity and asset value of real estate. Investors should look beyond short-term profitability and also consider the long-term value protected by such regulations.

## 6. The Challenge of Sustainability: Trends in Next-Generation Resorts Coexisting with Nature

The foundation of Karuizawa's asset value is, without a doubt, its rich natural environment. However, it is undeniable that the recent development rush and increase in tourists have placed a growing burden on this precious nature. For Karuizawa to continue developing as a sustainable resort destination toward 2030, a focus on "sustainability" is essential.

The town has already established strict landscape ordinances, building agreements, and guidelines for nature conservation. The designation "Urban planning zones outside of urbanization promotion and control areas" seen in the transaction data indicates that while these areas are not subject to development regulations under the City Planning Act, harmony with the natural environment is maintained through the town's own rules. This reflects the town's commitment to striking a balance between development and conservation.

In the future, proactive sustainability initiatives—such as the use of energy-efficient building materials, the adoption of renewable energy, and consideration for biodiversity—will likely influence real estate values. For example, energy-efficient homes utilizing geothermal or solar power, or villas designed with a high on-site greenery ratio to support the local ecosystem, could be highly valued by environmentally conscious buyers and the next generation of homeowners.

This also presents a new opportunity for investors. As ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) investing becomes a global trend, environmentally high-performing properties may benefit from more favorable financing terms from financial institutions or command a premium upon future resale. A "sustainability" perspective—one that considers not just immediate returns but whether a property can coexist with Karuizawa's natural environment and continue to generate value over the long term—will be the key to successful Karuizawa real estate investment going forward.

## 7. Risks for Investors to Watch: Oversupply, Infrastructure Issues, and Climate Change

While we have focused on the positive aspects of the Karuizawa real estate market, any investment requires a thorough risk assessment. Looking toward 2030, here are three points investors should be wary of.

First is the risk of oversupply. Spurred by the current boom, the development of villas and condominiums is proceeding at a rapid pace. If supply continues to outstrip the growth in demand, it could lead to price competition and a rise in vacancy rates in the future. In particular, generic properties lacking unique character are more susceptible to value depreciation when the market enters a correction phase.

Second are infrastructure issues. Traffic congestion, especially during the summer tourist season and long holidays, is a long-standing challenge for Karuizawa. The growing population is also placing an increased burden on essential services like water, sewage, and waste disposal. The number of medical facilities in the surveyed area is limited to three (including Kitazawa Clinic), which is sparse compared to urban centers. When considering permanent residency, the capacity of such life-sustaining infrastructure must be taken into account.

Third, and most critically, is the often-overlooked risk of natural disasters associated with climate change. An alarming fact was revealed in the hazard data analysis by Mekiki Research for this report. The area around Karuizawa Station is at risk of flooding, with a predicted maximum inundation depth of "5 to 10 meters" (Rank 4). This is an extremely high-risk level, indicating the potential for water to reach above the second floor of buildings.

While the risk of landslides is currently assessed as "none," the recent increase in the frequency of torrential downpours means that properties on sloped land or along streams require careful, individual investigation. Before purchasing real estate, investors must check the detailed risk zones on the hazard maps published by the local government and, if necessary, take maximum precautions, such as commissioning a soil survey by a specialist. Investors must never forget that Karuizawa's beautiful nature can, at times, bare its fangs.
