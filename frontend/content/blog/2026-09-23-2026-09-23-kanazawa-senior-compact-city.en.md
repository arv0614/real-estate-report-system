Kanazawa City has adopted "harmony between preservation and development" as its core urban planning philosophy, designating the arterial route from Kanazawa Port, through Kanazawa Station, down to Katamachi as the "urban axis." The compact city strategy of concentrating commercial, business, and residential functions along this axis is currently bearing significant fruit.

What deserves special attention is the generous residential support program uniquely deployed by Kanazawa City. Its flagship initiative, the "Kanazawa City Downtown/Residential Attraction Area Housing Acquisition Incentive," subsidizes a certain percentage of mortgage loans—up to 2 million yen when factoring in bonuses for child-rearing families and new migrants—when purchasing or building a house or condo in the downtown area. Furthermore, an additional subsidy of up to 500,000 yen is available under the "Kanazawa City District Plan Area Housing Acquisition Incentive," proving that the local government is absolutely serious about driving population flow back to the city center.

Looking at the actual data acquired this time through Mekiki Research, this area is zoned for "commercial use," allowing for high-density utilization with an 80% building coverage ratio and a 400% floor area ratio. Because developers can efficiently use limited land to construct mid-to-high-rise condominiums and mixed-use buildings, it is a highly attractive area for real estate development. As a result, an ideal virtuous cycle has emerged, making it easier to supply the high-quality, barrier-free housing stock that seniors demand.

## 7. Key Points for Identifying Prime Properties Where You Can "Live Safely for a Long Time" (For Homebuyers & Investors)

| Hazard Risks in Central Kanazawa (Must Check Before Purchase) |
| :--- |
| Flood Risk: Present (Max expected inundation depth rank 3: 3 to 5 meters) |
| Landslide Risk: Present (Some areas noted for potential debris flows) |

While downtown living in Kanazawa is undeniably attractive, there is one point you must never compromise on when choosing a property: "preparedness for natural disaster risks."

According to the hazard information we obtained, the target area carries both flood risks (with an expected maximum inundation of 3 to 5 meters) and landslide (debris flow) risks. Kanazawa boasts two beautiful rivers, the Sai River (犀川) and the Asano River (浅野川), which offer excellent scenery but also mean the city is not immune to flood risks during heavy rains. In areas where 3 to 5 meters of flooding is projected, detached houses (especially single-story homes or layouts with living spaces on the ground floor) come with a significant risk of losing your foundation for daily life.

Therefore, the ironclad rules for identifying a prime property where you can live safely for a long time are as follows:
1. For condominiums, choose units on the 2nd floor or higher (preferably 3rd floor or higher): In the unlikely event of flooding, you can protect your living space and valuable belongings.
2. Check the location of the building's electrical equipment (power receiving and transforming facilities): Condos with electrical equipment in the basement risk total power and water outages during a flood. Choose properties where these facilities are located slightly elevated on the first floor or on upper floors.
3. Avoid debris flow warning zones: Always check the hazard map. Exercise caution when considering properties near mountain slopes or cliffs, even if the price seems like a bargain.

Properly understanding hazard information and adopting the stance of "smartly choosing a property with a structure that avoids or mitigates risk"—rather than giving up and thinking, "I won't buy because there's a risk"—is the fundamental key to successful homebuying and real estate investment.
