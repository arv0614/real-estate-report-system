## 1. What Makes a City "Safe and Livable for the Long Term"? A Thought for Respect for the Aged Day

As we age and enter our senior years, what kind of environment truly qualifies as a "city where we can live safely for the long term"? In the past, a detached house with a large garden in the suburbs was considered the ideal home. However, once a household shrinks to just a couple, or driving becomes difficult, an overly large house or a location far from the station can actually become a burden on daily life.

What is required in this new era is a "highly convenient city" where daily shopping can be done within walking distance, and a trip to the family doctor is just a smooth bus or train ride away. This kind of environment is also highly attractive to younger families, offering easy commutes to work or school and effortless family outings to shopping centers on weekends.

Balancing "peace of mind" for seniors with "convenience" for the working-age population is a challenge that many regional cities are struggling with. Yet, Utsunomiya City has been quick to take concrete action, and is already demonstrating visible, successful results.

## 2. The Full Picture of Utsunomiya's "Networked Compact City"

Utsunomiya is striving toward an urban development model known as a "networked compact city." This ambitious plan aims to maintain the city's vitality despite a declining and aging population by consolidating essential lifestyle functions (medical, commercial, administrative, etc.) into specific hubs, and connecting these hubs via a robust public transportation network of LRT lines and transit buses.

For example, central Utsunomiya is designated as a "Commercial Zone," allowing for high-density land use with a building coverage ratio of 80% and a floor area ratio of 600%. This zoning paves the way for the efficient placement of commercial facilities and high-rise condominiums, laying the groundwork for a highly convenient lifestyle for a large number of residents.

## 3. How the Introduction of the LRT Created a "Walkable" Living Environment

The beating heart of Utsunomiya's compact city vision is its next-generation streetcar system, the LRT (Lightline). The eastern section, running from the east exit of JR Utsunomiya Station to the Haga Takanezawa Industrial Park (芳賀・高根沢工業団地), opened in August 2023. It has been a massive nationwide success, surpassing 10 million passengers in just under two years.

Now, the city's expectations are shifting toward the "westward extension." According to the latest plan announced by Utsunomiya City in October 2025, a target opening date of March 2036 has been set for an approximately 4.9-kilometer stretch. This new section will run from JR Utsunomiya Station, through the downtown area around Tobu Utsunomiya Station (東武宇都宮駅), and out to Kyoiku Kaikan-mae (教育会館前) near Komanyu 1-chome (駒生1丁目).

Initially aimed for a 2030 opening, the schedule was revised to ensure safe and reliable construction, accommodating land compensation for road widening and the relocation of underground utilities. Nevertheless, this project—representing a total investment of roughly 70 billion yen and adding 12 new stops—remains a powerful growth driver that will fundamentally elevate property values in the western district. In just ten years, the "walkable" environment—where even seniors without cars can hop on the LRT and smoothly access downtown—will expand across the entire western area.

## 4. Enhanced Livability for Seniors Through Better Access to Medical and Commercial Facilities

In a "walkable city," seniors place a particularly high premium on access to healthcare. According to our data, the area centered around Tobu Utsunomiya Station boasts a remarkable concentration of 45 medical facilities.

Specifically, everything from hospitals offering comprehensive care like Sato Hospital (佐藤病院) and Hara Eye Hospital (原眼科病院), to specialized clinics like Ai Kokoro Clinic (アイ・こころのクリニック), is available within walking distance or via public transit. The ability to maintain an independent lifestyle without having to rely on family members for rides, even when frequent medical visits become necessary in old age, is invaluable.

Furthermore, Tobu Utsunomiya Station on the Tobu Railway (東武鉄道) handles 9,861 passengers daily, and is surrounded by department stores and covered shopping arcades. The presence of commercial infrastructure that allows seniors to stay active—from daily grocery runs to leisurely weekend shopping—is another major strength of this area.

## 5. Great for Families and Singles Too! The Multigenerational Appeal of the LRT Corridor

Utsunomiya's appeal extends far beyond the senior demographic. The city also offers exceptionally generous support and an excellent educational environment for families raising children.

Data shows that the area is home to schools such as Showa Elementary School (昭和小学校) and Yohoku Junior High School (陽北中学校), ensuring a solid educational foundation. Even more noteworthy are Utsunomiya's comprehensive child-rearing support programs.
For instance, in Utsunomiya, out-of-pocket medical expenses for both outpatient visits and hospitalizations are completely free for children until the end of March of their 18th year (high school age), with no income caps. Additionally, through the Utsunomiya City Home Acquisition Support Subsidy (宇都宮市マイホーム取得支援事業補助金), families moving into designated residential promotion areas from outside the city receive generous housing subsidies of up to 850,000 yen (with an additional 50,000 yen per child). These and other related programs have been implemented and expanded for the 2025 and 2026 fiscal years.

For single residents, the vibrant dining and shopping options in the commercial areas around the stations, combined with excellent access to the city center, are major draws. Seniors, families, and singles—a city where multiple generations intersect and enjoy their unique lifestyles. This is the true vision of the compact city Utsunomiya is working to build.

As this timeline shows, major projects poised to boost the city's value are lined up every few years from 2026 to 2036. For investors, a highly effective strategy would be to secure prime properties now in the west exit area and along the extension route (such as Komanyumachi), in anticipation of the LRT extension and the completion of the 30-story mixed-use tower.

[*Crucial Hazard Risks to Confirm]
However, there is an important factor to confirm before making a purchase. According to hazard data retrieved from the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (国土地理院), parts of the target area are subject to flood risks (maximum inundation depth Rank 3: 3 to 5 meters) and sediment disaster (debris flow) risks.
To ensure long-term, safe living or to protect your investment property, checking hazard maps beforehand is absolutely essential. In areas prone to water damage, it is crucial to adopt a disaster-prevention mindset when selecting properties—such as choosing condo units on the second floor or higher (preferably the third floor or above) and verifying that electrical equipment is installed above ground level (for example, the aforementioned Atlas Tower Utsunomiya has mitigated this risk by locating its electrical room on the third floor).
