## 4. Accelerated Shift of Commercial Facility and Store Development Along the National Route 55 "Nangoku Bypass"

Following the shift in housing demand, the location of commercial facilities is also facing a major turning point. The primary recipient of this shift is the vicinity of the traffic artery, the National Route 55 "Nangoku Bypass."

Looking at the raw data, a wooden store built in 1970 (land and building) in the Kamisuematsu district (上末松地区) was traded for about 19 million yen. What is noteworthy is that this property is located in an "Urbanization Control Area (Building Coverage Ratio 60%, Floor Area Ratio 200%)." The fact that an existing commercial property is traded at this price in an Urbanization Control Area, where new development is restricted in principle, suggests the high commercial potential around the bypass. However, properties in such areas tend to have sparse public transaction data; therefore, careful comparison with neighborhood market prices and in-depth consultations with local authorities regarding the feasibility of rebuilding or hurdles to changing use are indispensable.

The infrastructure supporting this commercial demand is also well-established. The nearest station for the target area, "Gomen Station" operated by the Shikoku Railway Company, boasts an average of 3,642 daily passengers, functioning as an access hub to the center of Kochi City. In addition, medical institutions such as "Nishimura Dental Clinic (西村歯科医院)" are scattered around the area (one confirmed in this neighborhood sample), showing an increasing concentration of essential lifestyle facilities.

In regional cities with advanced motorization, areas along a bypass are "prime locations" for roadside stores. The trend of relocating and consolidating aging coastal stores along the bypass, driven by both disaster prevention and customer attraction needs, is likely to accelerate further in the future.

## 5. The Reality of Land Price Fluctuations Brought About by Collective Disaster Relocation Promotion Projects and Municipality-Led Infrastructure Development

The shift in a city's center of gravity is strongly propelled not only by the voluntary actions of residents and companies but also by government-led infrastructure development and collective disaster relocation promotion projects. Summarizing the timeline of recent development and relocation in Nangoku City looks like this:

- Late 2010s: Accompanying the revision of Nankai Trough Earthquake damage estimates, discussions on relocating functions inland gained momentum in parallel with the construction of tsunami evacuation towers in coastal areas.
- 2021–2023: Expansion of the road network around the Nangoku Bypass and land readjustment projects in inland areas progressed. From this period, the number of residential and commercial transactions in inland areas noticeably increased (reflected in our 630 transaction data points).
- 2024–2025: A succession of local companies relocating their offices and chain stores expanding from a wider region caused localized land price surges along the bypass.
- 2026 (Present): Disaster prevention infrastructure development has settled down, and the city's brand as a new "safe city" has taken root. The market has entered a phase of re-evaluating real estate values.

The ripple effects of such development are also evident in surrounding agricultural land transactions. In our data, 330㎡ of agricultural land in the Kamisuematsu district sold for about 500,000 yen, and 1,400㎡ of agricultural land in the Tamura district (田村地区) sold for about 130,000 yen. Although prices are extremely low due to restrictions under the Agricultural Land Act, developers might be actively maneuvering behind the scenes, eyeing future incorporation into Urbanization Promotion Areas or conversion into logistics and commercial land using special provisions.

## 7. Key "Property Evaluation" Points for B2B Real Estate Investment in the Nangoku City Area

As we have seen, Nangoku City is an attractive investment market undergoing dynamic transformation centered around the keyword "disaster prevention." However, to enter this market as a professional investor, you need to grasp several "key evaluation points":

1. Verification of Micro-Hazards: As mentioned earlier, the area as a whole harbors flood risks of 0.5 to 3 meters and debris flow risks. Even within the same neighborhood, a few dozen centimeters of elevation difference can separate success from failure. The meticulousness to check elevation data from the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan and past inundation records down to the block level is required.
2. Handling of Urbanization Control Areas: As seen in the Kamisuematsu district (1970-built store, approx. 19 million yen), transactions exist within Urbanization Control Areas. The source of value for these properties lies in whether they meet rebuilding permission requirements under the City Planning Act (such as certification as an existing residential lot). Neglecting preliminary consultations with local authorities risks being saddled with a non-performing asset.
3. Supplementing Data Blind Spots: In areas or property types where public transaction data is sparse, such as agricultural land or specialized business plots, it is dangerous to simply apply the average unit price (approx. 44,000 yen/㎡). Valuations through a multifaceted approach, including comparisons with neighborhood market prices and reverse calculations from roadside land prices, are essential.
