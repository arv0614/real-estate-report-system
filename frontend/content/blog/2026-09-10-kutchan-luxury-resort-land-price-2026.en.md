However, as of 2026, Kutchan Town has completely outgrown its framework as a mere "winter tourist destination." Fueled by the successive influx of foreign luxury hotels and the migration of wealthy individuals from both Japan and abroad, it has undergone a dramatic evolution into a "year-round luxury resort."

## 1. Introduction: The Current Landscape of the Ever-Evolving Kutchan Town (Niseko Area) in 2026

The frenzy once dubbed the "Niseko Bubble" has now transitioned into a "maturation phase as an international resort," backed by solid, actual demand. Behind the rising land prices are clear demand drivers.

Looking at Hokkaido as a whole, there is a wave of robust corporate expansion and infrastructure investment, such as the operation of large-scale data centers in the Ishikari (石狩) area and the advancement of the next-generation semiconductor industry—including Rapidus (ラピダス)—in the Chitose (千歳) area. This dramatic macroeconomic revitalization is boosting demand for second homes and "workation" properties in Kutchan Town among corporate executives and high-net-worth individuals from both within and outside Hokkaido, acting as an indirect yet powerful driver of land price appreciation.

What matters in investment decisions is not superficial price fluctuations, but whether "an ecosystem is being built where people live, work, and consume." Kutchan Town is currently in a phase where world-class service industries are clustering, creating new jobs, and prompting the rapid development of a corresponding residential environment.

## 2. The Influx of Foreign Luxury Hotels and the Shift Toward a "Year-Round" Luxury Resort

One of the biggest factors driving the Kutchan Town real estate market is the continuous entry of hotels by foreign luxury brands. Since Park Hyatt Niseko Hanazono (パーク ハイアット ニセコ HANAZONO) opened in 2020 and achieved success as a pioneer of the year-round resort concept, this trend has further accelerated from 2025 into 2026.

Looking just at recent developments, the following large-scale projects are underway or have been completed:
*   Chalet Ivy Weiss (シャレーアイビーワイス) (Opened Dec 2025): Opened in the Hanazono (花園) district with Hong Kong capital. Featuring a large natural hot spring bath, it has garnered attention as a facility aiming to attract both domestic and international guests year-round.
*   Mercure Niseko Resort (メルキュール ニセコ リゾート) (Scheduled to open Nov 2026): The global hotel group Accor (アコー) is making its first foray into the Niseko area by rebranding an existing facility.
*   Dhawa (DHAWA/ダワ) (Scheduled to open Autumn 2027): Full-scale construction has begun on this luxury hotel by the Singapore-based Banyan Group (バンヤン・グループ).

The arrival of these foreign hotels does more than just attract tourists; it generates a tremendous amount of local "employment." High-income professionals—from general managers and executive staff to restaurant chefs and multilingual concierges—are relocating to Kutchan Town from across Japan and around the world. As a result, demand is surging not only for luxury condominiums aimed at investors but also for high-quality rental housing and private homes for actual residents, forming a stable, year-round real estate market.

## 4. Not Just for Investment! Kutchan Town's Living Environment from the Perspective of End-Users (Homebuyers)

For end-users considering relocating or purchasing a home in Kutchan Town, a "safe living environment" and "daily infrastructure" are even more critical than price.

First, looking at environmental data, much of the urbanized area is designated as a "Category I Mid/High-Rise Exclusive Residential District" (60% building coverage ratio, 150% floor area ratio). This ensures a pleasant living environment with well-spaced mid-rise apartments and detached houses that benefit from ample sunlight and ventilation.

On the other hand, a must-check point before purchasing is hazard information (natural disaster risks).
According to actual data, certain areas in Kutchan Town have received a severe assessment indicating a "flood risk present (Maximum Inundation Depth Rank 4: 5–10 meters)." While the landslide disaster risk is assessed as "none," when purchasing or building a home along rivers or in low-lying areas, it is absolutely essential to consult hazard maps and incorporate flood mitigation measures during the design phase, such as placing living spaces on the second floor or higher.

Regarding essential medical facilities, actual data confirms four major clinics and facilities within the town, including Yotei Pediatric and Allergy Clinic (ようてい小児科・アレルギー科クリニック), Kutchan Azuma Eye Clinic (倶知安あづま眼科), and the Kutchan Town Health and Welfare Center (倶知安町保健福祉会館). Having pediatric and allergy clinics close at hand offers tremendous peace of mind for families.

## 5. Family Relocation: The Realities of International Schools, Healthcare, and Daily Shopping

One of the reasons Kutchan Town boasts immense popularity as a relocation destination for families is its "international educational environment," which ranks among the best in Japan, alongside generous "municipal childcare support."

While public schools such as Higashi Elementary School (東小学校) and Kutchan Junior High School (倶知安中学校) exist according to the data, the area is notably characterized by a remarkable concentration of international schools. In addition to the existing Hokkaido International School Niseko (北海道インターナショナルスクール ニセコ校), the new KIU Academy Niseko (KIUアカデミーニセコ校) is scheduled to open in April 2025. Furthermore, the CEA Group (CEAグループ), led by a former aide to the British Royal Family, is advancing a vast 224-hectare campus town concept in the nearby Weiss (ワイス) district. The brand of a "town where you can receive a world-class education while living in Japan" is rapidly being established.

Kutchan Town also provides highly comprehensive municipal support systems.
*   Childcare Support: The eligibility for children's medical expense subsidies has been expanded up to high school graduation. Furthermore, childcare fees are completely free starting from the first child, significantly reducing the financial burden on households.
*   Housing Subsidies: By utilizing the "Kutchan Town Housing Durability Improvement Renovation Subsidy," residents can receive up to 500,000 yen (20% of construction costs) for renovations such as exterior painting and insulation upgrades. For new builds, there is a "Kutchan-Style Housing Construction Subsidy," which includes an additional 100,000 yen for households with children, offering robust backing for homeownership.

Kutchan Town is an attractive option not only for families but also for singles and remote workers moving away from metropolitan centers.

On your days off, a drive of just a few tens of minutes takes you straight to the world's finest powder snow, or to summer outdoor activities like mountain biking, canoeing, and golfing. Liberation from packed commuter trains and the realization of a work-life balance that seamlessly transitions between "on" and "off" modes is the biggest reason remote workers choose this location.

## 7. Future Outlook: Transportation Infrastructure Development and Urban Planning Ahead of the Hokkaido Shinkansen Extension

When assessing mid-to-long-term asset value, the "extension of the Hokkaido Shinkansen" and the "redevelopment around Kutchan Station" are unavoidable topics.

In the Shinkansen extension project between Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto (新函館北斗) and Sapporo (札幌), Kutchan Station is planned as a crucial stop. Initially, the opening was scheduled for the "end of fiscal year 2030." However, due to difficulties in tunnel excavation, such as the emergence of massive boulders, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism has announced that the timeline may be delayed by several years, with "completion and opening now expected around the end of fiscal year 2038."

At first glance, this schedule delay might seem like negative news for the real estate market. However, from the perspective of professional investors, the exact opposite phenomenon is occurring.
A delay in the completion of the Shinkansen and large-scale redevelopment means that "new real estate supply will not increase easily (strong supply constraints)." As a result, the scarcity of existing prime locations and developed areas—where people are already active and commercial/lifestyle flows are established—actually increases. In fact, in the real estate market around Kutchan Station, rather than an acceleration of sell-offs, inquiries for selling and the rise in closing prices are still ongoing.

Future urban planning will proceed deliberately from a long-term perspective, based on a Location Normalization Plan that promotes a "Compact Plus Network" approach. The key to success is not buying speculatively based solely on artist's renderings, but making investment or purchase decisions by carefully evaluating current end-user demand and existing infrastructure.
