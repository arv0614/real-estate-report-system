## 1. 21 Years Since the TX Opened! The Current State of Kashiwa-no-ha Campus, Kashiwa City, Chiba Prefecture

The transportation infrastructure revolution brought about by the opening of the TX caused a paradigm shift in the real estate market from northwestern Chiba Prefecture to southern Ibaraki Prefecture. Among these areas, the vicinity of Kashiwa-no-ha Campus Station stands out as a symbol of railway-oriented development.

According to actual data, the daily number of passengers using this station has grown to approximately 24,200. Capitalizing on its exceptional commuting convenience—about 30 minutes to central Tokyo areas like Akihabara (秋葉原)—it has rapidly absorbed demand as a premier commuter town in the Tokyo metropolitan area.

However, a common "current challenge" faced by newly developed residential areas across Japan is the lag in infrastructure and environmental development amidst rapid population growth, along with the future risk of turning into aging "ghost towns" due to a declining birthrate and aging population. Simply developing a commuter town makes it difficult to maintain asset values decades down the line.

To address this challenge, the Kashiwa-no-ha area opted for a completely different approach: aiming to be a "sustainable, next-generation city" rather than "just a place to live." This vision forms the solid foundation that supports its robust asset value today.

## 2. The Core of the "Kashiwa-no-ha Smart City Concept" Driven by Public-Private-Academic Collaboration

When discussing the full scope of the Kashiwa-no-ha Campus redevelopment, the strong collaboration between the "public, private, and academic" sectors is indispensable. Chiba Prefecture and Kashiwa City (public), private enterprises led by Mitsui Fudosan (三井不動産), and academic institutions such as The University of Tokyo (東京大学) and Chiba University (千葉大学) have united to promote the Kashiwa-no-ha Smart City Concept (柏の葉スマートシティ構想) with a long-term perspective.

The core of this concept lies in three themes: "Environmental Coexistence," "Health and Longevity," and "New Industry Creation," functioning as a testing ground for cutting-edge technologies across the entire city.

For instance, the benefits are clearly visible in the educational environment. Around Toyofuta Elementary School (十余二小学校) and Tanaka Junior High School (田中中学校) within the area's school district, the ripple effects of next-generation education achieved through collaboration with academic institutions are highly anticipated. This has garnered strong support from education-conscious families and affluent households.

Such systematic urban planning, which goes beyond a fleeting boom, curbs real estate volatility (price fluctuation risk) and serves as a source of stable, long-term demand.

## 3. Realizing a Next-Generation Environmental City and a Dramatic Leap in Living Environment and Brand Power

Among the smart city initiatives, the efforts toward "Environmental Coexistence" and "Health and Longevity" directly enhance residents' quality of life (QOL) and dramatically elevate the area's real estate brand power.

An Area Energy Management System (AEMS) has been implemented, establishing a resilient and low-environmental-impact urban infrastructure that includes power sharing between city blocks. From an urban planning perspective, as shown in the acquired data, the area around the station is designated as a "Neighborhood Commercial Zone (80% building coverage, 300% floor area ratio)," ensuring an efficient and highly harmonized zoning of commercial facilities and residences.

Furthermore, in terms of "Health and Longevity," the comprehensive medical infrastructure is outstanding. According to actual data, the designated area is home to 18 medical institutions, ranging from large hospitals like Koukikai Tsujinaka Hospital Kashiwanoha (医療法人社団康喜会 辻仲病院柏の葉) to community-based clinics such as Miyamoto Family Clinic (みやもとファミリークリニック) and Nise Caps Clinic Kashiwanoha (医療法人社団ナイズ キャップスクリニック柏の葉).

An environment where advanced medical care and health promotion support are readily accessible daily strongly stimulates relocation demand not only from families seeking peace of mind but also from seniors with high purchasing power, robustly underpinning the area's asset value.

## 4. Land Price Trends from 2005 to 2026 and the Evolution of the Real Estate Market Along the Line

Since its opening in 2005, the real estate market along the TX line has consistently grown on an upward trajectory, weathering several economic shocks along the way.

According to the data acquired for the period between 2021 and 2025, the sample size of real estate transactions in the target area of Kashiwa City reached 8,267, proving it to be a highly liquid and active market.

Looking at the overall market average, the transaction price hovers around 36 million yen, with a median of 28 million yen. Additionally, the average unit price is approximately 140,000 yen/㎡, forming a solid market with room for growth compared to other areas in Chiba Prefecture.

What is particularly noteworthy is the range of transaction prices. An extreme spectrum has been recorded, from a minimum of about 1,000 yen to a maximum of about 4.5 billion yen. This is due to a mix of transactions—from intra-family share transfers and special leasehold land deals at the lower end, to large-scale business sites related to the smart city and massive commercial facility transactions at the upper end. It is a deep market where capital from diverse asset classes flows in.

## 6. Positioning Comparison with Neighboring Rival Areas (Nagareyama Otakanomori, etc.)

Along the TX line lies a formidable rival to Kashiwa-no-ha Campus: the neighboring station of Nagareyama Otakanomori (流山おおたかの森). Promoted as "the closest forest town to the city center," Nagareyama Otakanomori boasts explosive popularity as a commuter town for families, driven by thorough child-rearing support measures and a concentration of large commercial facilities.

In contrast, Kashiwa-no-ha Campus has established a uniquely edgy positioning focusing on "Academia, New Industry, and Smart City." If Nagareyama Otakanomori is a "city specialized in consumption and living," Kashiwa-no-ha Campus can be described as a "city where creation and research fuse."

From a real estate investor's perspective, this difference in character is significant. In a future where the birthrate continues to decline, a city strictly specialized for a specific child-rearing demographic is susceptible to risks from demographic shifts during generational turnovers. On the other hand, Kashiwa-no-ha Campus, with its concentration of university campuses, research facilities, and medical institutions, possesses a structure that sustainably attracts a diverse population, including students, researchers, medical professionals, and seniors. It offers highly attractive characteristics as a diversified destination for a real estate portfolio.

## 7. Expansion of Functions as a Hub for New Industry Creation and Future Investment Potential

The decisive factor for the future investment potential of Kashiwa-no-ha Campus is the expansion of its functions as a hub for "New Industry Creation." The area is equipped with incubation facilities, notably KOIL (Kashiwa-no-ha Open Innovation Lab, 柏の葉オープンイノベーションラボ), gathering a succession of cutting-edge startup companies in the fields of AI, IoT, and life sciences.

This ecosystem, where the advanced academic insights of The University of Tokyo and Chiba University intersect with private enterprise capital, generates new employment and promotes the influx of high-income knowledge workers. As a result, Kashiwa-no-ha Campus is evolving from a mere suburban commuter town into an independent urban model where "work, residence, academia, and recreation" exist in close proximity.

Such industrial agglomeration directly stimulates demand for offices as well as high-quality rental housing for singles and DINKS (Double Income, No Kids). In the future real estate market, one can fully expect not only demand for family-oriented residential condos but also improved profitability (stabilization of income gains) for investment residences, commercial properties, and office buildings.
