## 1. Why is Nagakute City, Aichi Prefecture Called "Japan's Youngest City"?

The primary reason Nagakute City is hailed as "Japan's youngest city" lies in its continuous population influx and the resulting overwhelmingly high proportion of young people and families. According to estimates from June 2026, the Nagakute District (長湫地区), which spans the western to southern parts of the city, exceeded a population of 50,000 on its own, showing that its growth speed knows no bounds.

This wave of population growth directly correlates with a booming real estate market. According to actual data retrieved from our media's backend system (such as the API from the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (国土交通省)), the number of real estate transaction samples in the target area from 2021 to 2025 reached 1,149. This is an exceptionally active transaction level for a commuter town near a regional core city.

The continuous influx of new residents stimulates a wide range of housing demand, from pre-owned condominiums to newly built detached houses, actively driving the metabolism of the entire city. For both prospective owner-occupiers looking to settle down long-term and real estate investors anticipating solid rental demand, a city with such a clear growth factor as "population increase" is an extremely rare find.

## 2. Nagakute City's Unique Childcare Support Measures that Attract Families

Robustly supporting Nagakute City's population growth are the generous childcare support measures led by the local government. As of 2026, Nagakute City is implementing unique policies that put it one step ahead of other municipalities.

Most notably, medical expenses (both outpatient and inpatient) are completely free of charge until the end of the fiscal year when a child turns 18 (high school age). Furthermore, a total of 100,000 yen equivalent is provided as a childbirth support grant, ensuring seamless financial support from pregnancy and childbirth through to high school graduation.

Additionally, starting in fiscal year 2026, the "Infant Nursery Attendance Support Program (Universal Children's Nursery System)" officially launched in nurseries within Nagakute City (such as Nagakute Higashi Nursery School (長湫東保育園)), preempting the government's new national system. This allows families with children between 6 months and under 3 years old to use childcare facilities flexibly, regardless of the parents' employment status, significantly contributing to alleviating the childcare burden not only for dual-income households but also for stay-at-home parent families.

Looking at actual living environment data, the target area is designated within the school zones for Nagakute Elementary School and Nagakute Junior High School, ensuring a well-established educational infrastructure. This enrichment of both soft (systems) and hard (school facilities) aspects serves as a powerful incentive, making families with children feel, "I want to live here."

## 3. The Evolution Along the Linimo Line! A Rich Living Environment Where Large Commercial Facilities and Parks Intersect

When discussing the appeal of Nagakute City, the dynamic urban development along the Tobu Kyuryo Line, known as "Linimo," is indispensable.

### Redevelopment Overview
Nagakute City is already home to a concentration of facilities with nationwide drawing power, such as Aeon Mall Nagakute (イオンモール長久手), IKEA Nagakute (IKEA長久手), and Ghibli Park (ジブリパーク) within the Aichi Earth Expo Memorial Park (愛・地球博記念公園). However, the city's evolution does not stop there.

In March 2025, a large supermarket themed as a "food theme park," Beisia Foods Park Nagakute Store (ベイシア Foods Park 長久手店), newly opened very close to the Linimo Koen Nishi Station (公園西駅). This marks the first new store opening in Aichi Prefecture in 13 years, dramatically improving the convenience of daily shopping. Furthermore, inside Aeon Mall Nagakute, commercial facility updates are continuously progressing, such as the new opening of a luxury purchasing shop Otakaraya (高価買取おたからや) in August 2026.

In addition, Nagakute Onsen Gozarasse (長久手温泉ござらっせ), long cherished as a relaxation spot, reopened with a new look in August 2026, now featuring four different types of saunas. Moreover, the basic plan for the Sports Forest Redevelopment Project (スポーツの杜再整備事業) promoted by Nagakute City has been finalized, moving forward with the development of an outdoor sports hub where multiple generations can interact. A living environment where commercial convenience seamlessly intersects with nature and leisure is precisely Nagakute City's greatest weapon.

While Nagakute City is full of charm, there are several points to keep in mind when actually purchasing a property. Looking closely at the actual data reveals factors causing price disparities even within the same region.

*   Higashiharayama: 3LDK / Built in 2017 / Quasi-Industrial District / Approx. 32 million yen (Area 80㎡)

Even for a 3LDK in the same district, differences in the year built (2017 vs. 1994) can result in a price gap of over 10 million yen. Also, what cannot be overlooked is the difference in zoning districts. Even within Higashiharayama, "Quasi-Industrial Districts" and "Category II Mid/High-Rise Residential Districts" coexist.

While quasi-industrial districts make it easier to build daily convenience facilities, there is also the possibility of factories and warehouses being situated nearby. Do you prioritize a quiet living environment, or convenience? Although the hazard risk is generally low in this area, such micro-location conditions (zoning districts, building coverage ratios, and floor area ratios) dictate day-to-day livability and future asset value. Before purchasing, it is crucial to always check the zoning district in the data and walk around the local area to experience the neighborhood's atmosphere firsthand.
