## 2. How is the Beautiful Night View Protected? Kobe City's Landscape Regulation System

| Examples of Districts/Zones in Actual Data | Zoning (用途地域) | BCR / FAR | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Otsukidai (大月台) | Category I Exclusively Low-Story Residential Zone | 40% / 80% | Strict height and area limits. Easy to secure views due to spacious lots. |
| Akasakadori (赤坂通) | Category I Mid/High-Rise Oriented Residential Zone (第1種中高層住居専用地域) | 60% / 200% | A mix of mid-rise condos and detached houses. A well-balanced living environment. |
| Izumidori (泉通) | Category I Residential Zone (第1種住居地域) | 60% / 200% | A convenient area where certain sizes of shops and facilities can be located alongside residences. |

The trouble of "a tall apartment building suddenly being built next door, blocking my precious view" is a frequent risk when purchasing real estate in urban areas. However, in the hilltop areas of Nada Ward, Kobe City, this risk is minimized through urban planning.

The key to this lies in strict building regulations based on Kobe City's uniquely designated Scenic Zones (風致地区) and the Zoning (用途地域) under the City Planning Act. Let's look at a sample of actual data. In the transaction records for "Otsukidai," located in the hillside/hilltop area of Nada Ward, a sprawling 350-square-meter property built in 2012 was traded for 98 million yen. Otsukidai is designated as a "Category I Exclusively Low-Story Residential Zone," imposing extremely strict limits: a Building Coverage Ratio (BCR) of 40% and a Floor Area Ratio (FAR) of 80%.
This means that the area and height of buildings that can be constructed relative to the lot size are strictly restricted. As a result, ample space is created between adjacent properties, forming a system where lush greenery, beautiful views, and nightscapes are preserved semi-permanently.

Furthermore, a large-scale redevelopment that merges city and nature is currently underway in Nada Ward. A "Green Plaza" featuring lawn areas, waterside spaces, and rows of cherry trees is scheduled to be developed. Just a short trip from the hilltop residential areas, an environment will be ready where you can refresh yourself in a vast park.
The fact that this "green network" is protected and continues to evolve greatly elevates the quality of life (QOL) of the residents, serving as a strong motivation to want to continue living there for a long time.

## 4. Benefit of Landscape Protection ②: Securing Sunlight and Ventilation

| Differences in Living Environments by Zoning (From Actual Data) | BCR | FAR | Impact on Living Environment (Benefits) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Category I Exclusively Low-Story Residential Zone (e.g., Otsukidai) | 40% | 80% | Extremely good sunlight and ventilation as a large garden can be secured and the distance from neighboring houses is far. |
| Category I Mid/High-Rise Oriented Residential Zone (e.g., Akasakadori) | 60% | 200% | Allows for the construction of 3-story detached houses and condos that effectively utilize the land while securing a certain amount of sunlight. |

Building regulations meant to protect views are actually directly linked to the fundamental comfort of a home, such as "sunlight" and "ventilation."
In areas with a 40% BCR, like the aforementioned Otsukidai, 60% of the lot must be left as open space (such as a garden or parking lot). Because buildings are not densely packed together, plenty of sunlight pours in through south-facing windows, and you can bring the pleasant breeze blowing down from Mount Rokko into your home.

On the other hand, looking at the sample data for "Akasakadori," located slightly further down the mountain, it is a "Category I Mid/High-Rise Oriented Residential Zone" with a 60% BCR and 200% FAR. Here, recently built detached houses (built in 2020–2021) on 55 to 70-square-meter lots are traded for about 39 to 48 million yen, or older properties (built in 1964) for 5.6 million yen. While it doesn't offer the same spaciousness as the strictly regulated hilltop areas, it is still a zoning area that protects the residential environment, allowing for a lifestyle that balances convenience and comfort.
Depending on your budget and lifestyle, determining how much "spaciousness (strictness of regulations)" you desire will be a crucial point in choosing a property.

Some might worry, "The hilltops have great views, but isn't daily life inconvenient?" However, when it comes to Nada Ward, there is no need for such concern.
As the actual data shows, JR "Rokkomichi Station," the transportation hub of the area, is a major station boasting 49,496 passengers a day. Commercial facilities are clustered around the station, meaning you will never struggle with daily shopping. Additionally, around the target area, there are 38 medical institutions of varying sizes, including "Yasuo-kai Nishi Hospital," "Iwakura ENT Clinic," and "Ueno Dermatology Clinic," providing a well-equipped medical infrastructure where everyone from singles to seniors can live with peace of mind.

Furthermore, in terms of the educational environment, there are public schools such as "Nishinada Elementary School" and "Takajo Junior High School," making it a popular school district for families passionate about education.
Especially noteworthy is the child-rearing support and housing subsidy program "Sumikaeru," which Kobe City continues to strongly promote in FY 2026. When young couples (combined age of 90 or under) or households with preschool children move for the purpose of living near or with their parents' household, they can receive a subsidy of up to 350,000 yen. It can also be used in conjunction with energy-saving renovation subsidies such as the national "Mirai Eco Housing 2026 Project" (みらいエコ住宅2026事業), making it a massive opportunity to acquire your ideal home while significantly reducing the financial burden.
