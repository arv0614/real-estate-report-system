## 3. Correlation Analysis Between Tsunami/Inland Flooding Hazards and Land Price Trends in the Shiroyama (城山) and Prefectural Office (県庁) Areas

Inundation risk goes hand-in-hand with the concentration of urban functions. According to actual data, specific areas in central Matsuyama City are classified as "at risk" for flood hazards, with the maximum estimated inundation depth reaching "Rank 3 (3–5 meters)."

As an analyst, I want to strongly caution real estate investors and prospective homebuyers about the gravity of this "3 to 5 meters" metric. For a standard detached house, this means being submerged up to the eaves of the second floor. Even for condominiums and commercial buildings, it means the first-floor entrance, pilotis, and underground electrical equipment rooms would be completely underwater. Before purchasing, it is essential not only to review the Important Matters Explanation Document (重要事項説明書) but also to personally walk the area and verify the micro-topography (elevation differences) alongside the hazard map.

Looking at the correlation with land price trends, price formation that prices in this risk is advancing in areas where inundation risk is evident. Particularly in Commercial Zones with a 600% FAR, developers constructing new buildings are compelled to implement water damage countermeasures (BCP measures), such as relocating electrical equipment to the roof or intermediate floors. The resulting increase in construction costs acts as downward pressure on land acquisition prices (raw land prices). On the flip side, newly built or relatively new properties equipped with adequate water-resistant infrastructure become rare commodities in the area, enjoying strong support from tenants and maintaining high unit rents.

## 6. Decoding the Gap Between Liquefaction Risks and Market Prices in Matsuyama City Using Mekiki Research

In a Nankai Trough Megaquake, the differential settlement of buildings caused by "liquefaction" is just as terrifying, if not more so, than the shaking itself. Ground liquefaction inflicts fatal damage, especially on heavy Reinforced Concrete (RC) structures.

From the actual data, let's examine a transaction case of a multi-family dwelling in Iwasakicho (岩崎町) (Category I Mid/High-Rise Oriented Residential Zone, BCR 60% / FAR 200%).
- Residential Land (Land and Building): 29 million yen (165 sqm, built in 1985, RC structure)

This RC multi-family dwelling, transacted at 29 million yen, was built in 1985. Since it was constructed under the "New Seismic Standards" implemented following the 1981 revision of the Building Standards Act (建築基準法), a certain level of earthquake resistance is guaranteed. However, now that it is approaching 40 years old, concerns arise over the carbonation of concrete and the progression of cracks accumulating from past minor earthquakes.

Even more critical is the ground risk. In the flatlands of Matsuyama City, the risk of liquefaction is not zero. The standards for ground surveys and piling in 1985 are sometimes deemed insufficient by today's latest insights. Given Matsuyama City's average unit price of roughly 80,000 yen/sqm, the fact that an RC multi-family dwelling of this size was traded for 29 million yen suggests that the potential risk premiums required for future large-scale repair costs, seismic retrofitting, and liquefaction countermeasures have been discounted (creating a gap) from the actual market price. Before jumping at a high gross yield, rigorous due diligence on ground risk is absolutely required.
