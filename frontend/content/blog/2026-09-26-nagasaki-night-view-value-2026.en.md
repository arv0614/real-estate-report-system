## 3. What Are the "Landscape Regulations" That Protect These Stunning Views? (A Beginner's Guide)

| BCR and FAR by Zoning (Examples from Nagasaki City Data) | Building Coverage Ratio (BCR) | Floor Area Ratio (FAR) | General Idea of Main Restrictions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Category I Exclusively Low-Rise Residential Zone (Ote, Aburagimachi, etc.) | 50% | 100% | Primarily spacious detached houses up to about two stories. Tall buildings are prohibited. |
| Category I Exclusively Mid/High-Rise Residential Zone (Ozonemachi, Izumi, etc.) | 60% | 200% | Mid-rise condos and apartments can be built. Subject to sunlight regulations. |
| Commercial Zone (Ienomachi, City Center, etc.) | 80% | 400–600% | Densely packed with office buildings and high-rise condos. Mixed with commercial facilities. |

The greatest contributors to preserving Nagasaki City's beautiful night views and excellent living environments are the "landscape regulations" and "zoning restrictions" enforced by the local government. Though it might seem complicated with all the technical jargon, these are essentially rules stating, "You cannot construct buildings beyond a certain height or size in this area."

For instance, Ote and Aburagimachi from our actual data examples are designated as "Category I Exclusively Low-Rise Residential Zones," where the building coverage ratio (the percentage of the building footprint relative to the lot size) is limited to 50%, and the floor area ratio (the ratio of total floor area to lot size) is capped at 100%. This means that "half of the lot must be left as a yard or open space, and you can only build a house of about two stories at most." Thanks to these strict limitations, the risk of a massive condo suddenly being built next door and stealing your sunlight and view is extremely low.

On the other hand, in a "Category I Exclusively Mid/High-Rise Residential Zone" like Izumi (BCR 60%, FAR 200%), the construction of mid-rise apartments and condos is permitted, but designs that are considerate of the surrounding environment are still required.

What would happen if everywhere had rules like a "Commercial Zone" (BCR 80%, FAR 400–600%), where tall buildings could be constructed freely? Nagasaki's beautiful sloping terrain would be overrun with sterile high-rise buildings, ruining both the night views and the natural scenery. Landscape regulations might initially seem like an obstacle to development, but they are actually "a powerful shield that protects the existing beautiful views and living environments for eternity."

## 4. Why Landscape Regulations Prolong the "Livability" of Your Home

| Hazard Risks in Nagasaki City (Alerts Based on Actual Data) | Risk Assessment | Details & Points of Caution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flood Risk | Present | Max inundation depth Rank 4 (5–10m). Caution required in lowlands and along rivers. |
| Landslide Risk | Present | Phenomena such as debris flows. Steep slopes and hilltops with retaining walls require confirmation. |

The benefits brought by landscape regulations go far beyond just "having a good view." Rules that prevent building congestion and ensure open spaces guarantee abundant sunlight and ventilation. Because houses situated on slopes can take in light and wind over the roofs of the buildings in front of them—much like terraced fields—they are less prone to trapping moisture, which helps prolong a healthy and comfortable living space.

However, when choosing a home on a hilltop or slope, checking the hazard (disaster) risks is something that, as a professional analyst, I must emphasize cannot be ignored.

As clearly shown in the actual data, the central and lowland areas of Nagasaki City have a very high flood risk, categorized as "Max inundation depth Rank 4 (5–10m)." In this regard, hilltop areas offer a viable option for protecting life and property from water damage. On the flip side, however, there are quite a few locations identified as having risks unique to hilltops, such as "landslides (e.g., debris flows)."

Do not jump at a property simply because "the view is great." Always make sure to check the latest hazard map before purchasing. Carefully inspecting whether the property is designated as a Landslide Warning Area (Yellow Zone or Red Zone) and checking retaining walls (the concrete walls supporting the slope) for cracks or deterioration alongside an expert is an absolute prerequisite for a long, worry-free life there.

When families choose a home, they place just as much importance on the "child-rearing environment" as they do on views and prices. Nagasaki City's hilltop areas offer a well-balanced fusion of nature and urban functions, providing an extremely attractive environment for the child-rearing generation.

For instance, the surrounding target area is home to historic schools such as Oura Elementary School and Umegasaki Junior High School, where a calm and stable educational environment can be expected. Furthermore, medical facilities are highly abundant; there are 134 medical institutions in the vicinity alone, including Nagasaki Jin Hospital, Dejima Hospital, and Kobayashi Hospital. The peace of mind that comes with knowing you can immediately access proper medical care in the event of an injury or sudden illness is a huge plus for families raising children.

Regarding transportation access, Shinchi Chinatown Station on the streetcar line (Nagasaki Electric Tramway) boasts 13,430 daily passengers and serves as a central hub for commuting to major spots within the city. The route from the hilltops to the stations at the base (navigating the sloped terrain) has been a longstanding challenge, but in recent years, convenience has improved dramatically due to the expansion of community buses supporting the hillside areas and the widespread use of electric-assist bicycles.

Moreover, as of 2026, Nagasaki City has rolled out generous subsidy programs aimed at encouraging the child-rearing generation to settle down. A prime example is the Nagasaki City Child-rearing Housing Support Subsidy (長崎市子育て住まいづくり支援費補助金). When households with multiple children or child-rearing families renovate an existing home, or purchase and renovate one, they can receive up to 400,000 to 500,000 yen in subsidies, with an additional 100,000 yen awarded for utilizing a vacant house. Additionally, by taking advantage of national partnerships like the Mirai Eco Housing 2026 Project (みらいエコ住宅2026事業), it is possible to acquire a comfortable home with high insulation performance at a great value. The ability to fully leverage these systems to realize your ideal living environment while keeping initial costs low is a major strength of the current real estate market in Nagasaki City.

When forecasting future asset values, you cannot overlook the timeline of the large-scale redevelopment currently underway in central Nagasaki City—often referred to as a "once-in-a-century" undertaking.

Nagasaki Stadium City, spearheaded by the Japanet Group (ジャパネットグループ) and opened in the fall of 2024, functions not merely as a sports facility but as a massive city-within-a-city encompassing an arena, hotel, retail spaces, and offices. A grand 2nd-anniversary festival is scheduled for October 2026, and it has already completely established itself as an "everyday oasis" for the citizens of Nagasaki.

How to spread this downtown vibrancy to the surrounding areas is the current overarching theme for Nagasaki City. Looking ahead to 2028–2030, the establishment of "walkable zones" emphasizing pedestrian flow will advance based on the Nagasaki City Center Urban Development Concept. Furthermore, plans are in place to widen the arterial roads alongside Nagasaki Station, aiming for completion in FY 2032.

The implications of these developments for the real estate market are profound. As the city center's infrastructure is upgraded and walkability improves, the convenience of the "hilltop residential areas" located just a short distance from the center will skyrocket. Areas that were previously shunned because "the views are nice but access to downtown is a bit inconvenient" are now transforming into the ultimate hybrid locations: places where you can easily pop out to Stadium City or the commercial facilities in front of the station, and then return to a quiet hilltop home with a stunning night view. The timing for this transformation is right now.
