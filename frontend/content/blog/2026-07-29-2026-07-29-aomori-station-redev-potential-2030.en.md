## 1. The Challenge of a Declining Population: Aomori City's Vision for its Urban Future

The demographic challenges of population decline and a super-aging society, faced by many of Japan's regional cities, create serious issues such as difficulties in maintaining public services and a decline in regional economic vitality. Aomori City is no exception, and fundamental solutions have been sought for sustainable urban management. The city's answer is the "Compact City" model.

At the heart of this strategy is the principle of "selection and concentration." Attempting to maintain sprawling urban areas as they are would lead to increased infrastructure costs for roads, water, and snow removal, making public services inefficient. Therefore, the city is concentrating urban functions such as residential areas, commerce, healthcare, and welfare in specific areas, particularly the central urban district with its excellent public transportation access. This allows citizens to enjoy a comfortable lifestyle on foot or via public transit, without being overly reliant on cars. Simultaneously, it enables the administration to efficiently allocate its limited financial resources and continue providing high-quality services.

Aomori City's challenge is not merely about shrinking the city but about revitalizing the city as a whole by enhancing the appeal of its central core to attract people. This grand vision is the driving force behind the Aomori Station area redevelopment projects we will introduce next.

## 2. The Full Scope of the Aomori Station Area Redevelopment: The Mid-Life Tower and Auga Revitalization Plan

Symbolizing Aomori's Compact City strategy are several large-scale redevelopment projects currently in progress around Aomori Station. These are set to transform the landscape of the station-front area and are expected to act as a catalyst for generating new foot traffic.

JR Aomori Station East Exit Building Development:
Serving as the new "face" of the station, this project involves a mixed-use building featuring commercial facilities, a hotel, and municipal government functions. Being directly connected to the station, a major transportation hub, the facility will be highly convenient not only for residents but also for tourists and business travelers, and will dramatically enhance the station area's role as a hub.

Mid-Life Tower Aomori Ekimae:
This is a public-private mixed-use tower that will house condominiums, commercial facilities, and the Aomori Citizens' Art Exhibition Hall. Particularly noteworthy is its provision of new residential options in the central urban area. It will absorb demand from those seeking the convenience of downtown living, especially active seniors, DINKs (Dual Income, No Kids), and singles, playing a key role in promoting a population return to the city center.

Auga Revitalization Plan:
The "Auga" (アウガ) complex, once a mixed-use commercial facility run by a public-private corporation, is being reborn as a complex housing part of the main city hall functions and the "Kasaisen" (カサイセン) market. By integrating administrative functions with commercial services closely tied to residents' daily lives, it is expected to attract a steady flow of people, contributing to the vibrancy of the surrounding area.

These projects are not independent but work in synergy with the common goal of concentrating diverse urban functions—commercial, residential, administrative, and cultural—in the central district. This functional concentration is the most critical factor in redefining real estate value in Aomori City.

## 3. The City's "Compact City Plus Network" Strategy Explained

Aomori City's urban strategy is not just a "Compact City." The addition of the phrase "Plus Network" is crucial. This concept involves connecting the "hubs" (the compact city) where urban functions are concentrated with residential areas and community centers scattered in the suburbs via an efficient public transportation system (the network).

The impact of this strategy on the real estate market will vary significantly by area.

*   Central Urban Area (Hubs): With the concentration of commercial, medical, and administrative services and improved transportation convenience, these areas will become more attractive for residential living. Properties within walking distance of the station or along major bus routes are particularly expected to see an increase in asset value. As the population ages, an environment where one can live without a car will be a significant added value.
*   Suburban Community Hubs: Certain suburban areas serving as public transit nodes may maintain a certain level of value as centers of local communities. Areas with clusters of supermarkets and clinics will likely continue to see demand.
*   Suburban Areas Far from Hubs: On the other hand, areas outside the public transport network with few amenities will likely experience population drain and strong downward pressure on property values. The increase in vacant homes is also a concern, requiring cautious judgment for investment purposes.

In short, the "Compact City Plus Network" strategy will lead to a clear "sorting" of property values within the city. Investors need to accurately understand the city's vision for the future and identify which areas are designated as "hubs." On top of that, a detailed analysis of concrete real estate transaction data becomes essential.

## 5. Potential as a Transportation Node: Strengthening Links with Shinkansen and the Port

The value of Aomori Station extends beyond being merely the center of city transportation. Its role as a hub for wide-area transportation is crucial for forecasting future real estate values.

Linkage with the Hokkaido Shinkansen:
Aomori Station, connected by conventional railway lines to Shin-Aomori Station—the gateway to the Hokkaido Shinkansen—is an important gateway connecting Honshu and Hokkaido. Once the extension to Sapporo is complete, its role will become even more significant. Shinkansen passengers, especially tourists from within Japan and abroad, will have more opportunities to visit the city center via Aomori Station, boosting demand for hotels, commercial facilities, and restaurants.

Linkage with the Port of Aomori (Tsugaru Kaikyo Ferry):
The Port of Aomori serves as a ferry terminal connecting to Hakodate (函館) and is a hub for both logistics and passenger traffic. The geographical advantage of the station and port being in close proximity makes it easy to create tourist itineraries and holds great potential for capturing inbound tourism demand.

A Note on the Data:
In this survey's data, the nearest station information (`environment.station.name`) is `null`. This is likely because the survey point is Aomori Station itself or due to the specifications of the data acquisition. However, this macro-level perspective of a "wide-area transportation hub," which doesn't appear in the data, is the very foundation that will support the long-term value of real estate around Aomori Station.

If a synergistic effect is created between the enhanced appeal of the station area from redevelopment and these wide-area transportation networks, there is no doubt it will have a positive impact on rents and occupancy rates for commercial properties, and ultimately on real estate prices themselves.

## 6. Predicting the Transformation of Commercial and Residential Areas: The Impact of Functional Concentration in the City Center

As the compact city strategy progresses, the landscape of Aomori's commercial and residential areas is projected to be significantly redrawn.

Population Return to the City Center:
The supply of convenient high-rise condominiums and rental housing through redevelopment will accelerate the trend of some residents, who previously lived in detached houses in the suburbs, moving back to the city center. The living environment around the station is highly appealing, especially for elderly individuals for whom driving is becoming a burden and for younger people and singles seeking a short commute. The data confirms the presence of 38 medical institutions (`environment.medicalCount`) in the vicinity of the survey area, and good access to facilities like Murakami Shinmachi Hospital (村上新町病院) and Aoi Umi Koen Clinic (青い海公園クリニック) provides significant peace of mind, particularly for seniors.

Concentration and Revitalization of Commercial Functions:
The commercial center will shift from suburban roadside stores towards redeveloped buildings in front of the station and central shopping districts. An increase in the residential population will create a new customer base for daily consumption, stimulating businesses in sectors like dining, retail, and services to open new locations. This will transform the city center into a vibrant space bustling with activity on both weekdays and weekends, potentially pushing up rental levels and asset values for retail properties.

The Importance of School Districts:
For families with children, the school district is also a critical factor. The survey point is located within the Okidate Elementary School (沖館小学校, `environment.schools.elementary`) district. Even in the city center, the availability of such educational facilities is a positive factor in attracting diverse households.

However, this transformation will create both light and shadow. Behind the concentration of people, goods, and money in the center lies the risk of worsening hollowing-out and vacant home problems in suburban areas. Accurately reading this trend of "polarization" is the key to successful real estate investment.

## 7. Investment Focus: Areas and Asset Types Poised for Value Growth

Based on the analysis so far, we propose the following specific points of focus for real estate investment, looking ahead to Aomori City in 2030.

Key Areas to Watch:
1.  Within a 10-minute walk of Aomori Station: This area will most directly benefit from the redevelopment. High demand is expected for both commercial and residential use, offering the greatest potential for asset value appreciation.
2.  Convenient areas along major bus routes: These areas are central to the city's "Network" strategy. Locations with good access to the city center and equipped with supermarkets and medical facilities can expect stable rental demand.

Key Asset Types to Consider:
1.  Compact condominiums for singles and DINKs: These will be the primary housing choice for the population returning to the city center. Not only new constructions but also older properties targeted for renovation show promise.
2.  Serviced housing for the elderly (Sa-ko-ju): An asset class that captures the trends of an aging population and the demand for urban living. Collaboration with medical institutions is key. Given the presence of 38 medical facilities in the survey area, the locational advantage is high.
3.  Ground-floor retail and small-scale commercial buildings: With the increase in residential population, demand from tenants targeting daily consumption will rise. Inquiries from service industries such as restaurants, clinics, and beauty salons are expected to increase.

The Biggest Investment Risk Factor: Hazard Information
However, when considering an investment, one factor that absolutely cannot be overlooked is the risk of natural disasters. According to the Mekiki Research data, a flood risk exists at the survey point, with a maximum expected inundation depth of "3 to 5 meters" (`hazard.flood.maxDepthRank: 3`). This is a level at which the first floor of a building would be completely submerged, representing an extremely high risk. On the other hand, no landslide risk has been identified (`hazard.landslide.hasRisk: false`).

When acquiring real estate, it is essential to check detailed risks on the hazard maps published by the local government and thoroughly investigate the building's structure (e.g., whether it is a flood-resistant RC structure), insurance coverage, and evacuation routes. An investment that ignores disaster risk can lead to catastrophic losses, no matter how profitable it may seem.
