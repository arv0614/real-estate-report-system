## 1. Introduction: Why is the Sapporo Odori/Susukino Area a Hotspot Now?

The Odori and Susukino area, the very heart of Sapporo, is currently in a period of major transformation. The primary catalyst for this change is the bid to host the 2030 Winter Olympics and Paralympics. If the bid is successful, infrastructure development and urban renewal will advance by leaps and bounds, undoubtedly drawing significantly more attention from both domestic and international audiences. Fueled by this anticipation, large-scale, private-sector-led redevelopment projects are already underway, poised to dramatically enhance the city's potential.

In particular, the area where Sapporo Ekimae Dori (Station Avenue) and Odori Park intersect is becoming the stage for mixed-use urban development that integrates offices, commercial facilities, hotels, and residences. A succession of plans to reinvent former landmarks has been announced, stimulating corporate investment appetite. This is more than just replacing old buildings; it's a movement to generate new foot traffic and redefine the brand value of the entire area.

From an investor's perspective, this period of transformation presents a major opportunity. Improvements in urban functions, transportation convenience, and living environments resulting from redevelopment directly translate into higher real estate asset values. In this article, we will take a deep dive into the real estate market around Sapporo Odori Park, the epicenter of this dynamic change, using concrete data.

## 2. The 2030 Winter Olympics Bid and Its Impact on the Real Estate Market

The bid for the 2030 Winter Olympics is a topic of paramount interest for Sapporo's real estate market. As seen in past host cities, real estate prices tend to fluctuate significantly from the moment the host city is announced through the post-Games period.

First, if the bid is successful, infrastructure investment will accelerate. The construction and renovation of competition venues, as well as the enhancement of transportation networks and accommodation facilities, will be advanced through public-private partnerships. This will boost demand in the construction sector and invigorate related industries. In the real estate market, expectations for land price appreciation will rise, especially in areas around the planned sites for the athletes' village and media center, and locations with good access to major stations. Furthermore, the global attention will enhance Sapporo's brand image, which could also stimulate real estate investment from high-net-worth individuals both in Japan and abroad.

On the other hand, the bidding process is not without its uncertainties. If the bid is unsuccessful, there's a risk that real estate investment, previously driven by anticipation, could temporarily stagnate. In particular, hotel development and commercial facility plans predicated on hosting the Olympics may be subject to review.

However, it is important to note that the current redevelopment in central Sapporo is not solely dependent on the Olympics. At its core, the movement is driven by the need to address more fundamental urban challenges, such as the recovery of inbound tourism and the renewal of aging buildings. Therefore, regardless of the Olympic bid's outcome, central Sapporo's potential will not be diminished. Investors need a level-headed perspective that avoids over-reliance on the major event and instead focuses on the intrinsic value created by the enhancement of urban functions through redevelopment.

## 3. Deep Dive into Major Redevelopment Projects ①: The Sapporo Kokusai Building Site and Surrounding Area

A project symbolizing the redevelopment of the Odori area is the plan to rebuild the Sapporo Kokusai Building, which faces Odori Park. On this prime location, a 34-story, 185-meter-tall mixed-use skyscraper is planned, which is expected to house offices, commercial facilities, and a luxury hotel on its upper floors. This project will not only bring a new building to the area but will also significantly alter the skyline along Odori Park and become a landmark that will drive the value of the entire area.

The feasibility of such a large-scale development is rooted in the area's unique urban planning characteristics. According to data from "Mekiki Research" for the Sapporo Odori Park vicinity, the area is zoned as a "Commercial Zone," with a building coverage ratio (BCR) of 80% and a floor area ratio (FAR) of 800%. This indicates that high-density land use is permitted, creating an environment conducive to constructing tall, large-scale buildings. The Sapporo Kokusai Building site project is a development that takes full advantage of this potential.

Furthermore, activity is buzzing in the surrounding area as well. The nearby Arche Building is also being considered for reconstruction, and this chain reaction of redevelopment is expected to transform the Odori area into a new hub for business and tourism. The supply of new office floors will help attract new companies, while the commercial facilities and hotels will bustle with visitors from Japan and abroad. These developments are highly likely to have a positive impact on surrounding office and retail rents, and by extension, on land prices in residential areas.

## 4. Deep Dive into Major Redevelopment Projects ②: The Ripple Effect of "COCONO SUSUKINO"

The Susukino area, one of Japan's premier entertainment districts located south of Odori, has also undergone a significant transformation through redevelopment. The symbol of this change is the mixed-use commercial facility "COCONO SUSUKINO," which opened in November 2023. Built on the former site of the Susukino Lafiler building, this complex features a supermarket, a diverse range of restaurants, a cinema complex, and a hotel, and is poised to redefine the traditional image of Susukino.

The opening of COCONO SUSUKINO has successfully attracted a new demographic to the area. Families, young people, and tourists now visit during the day, and the district is evolving from its traditional image as a nightlife hub into an entertainment destination enjoyable day and night. This change is having a positive effect on the surrounding real estate market.

First, it is likely to increase demand for commercial retail space, leading to a decrease in vacancy rates and a rise in rental prices. The improved image of the area is also spilling over into demand for nearby condominiums (known as "mansions" in Japan). For those seeking an urban lifestyle, the appeal of the Susukino area, with its blend of daily conveniences and entertainment, is growing. This is expected to vitalize the pre-owned condominium market and could act as a catalyst for new condominium development. The "rejuvenation" of the district brought about by redevelopment is creating a virtuous cycle that pushes up real estate values.

## 6. Inbound Tourism Recovery and the State of Hotel Development

Inbound tourism demand (from foreign visitors), which had temporarily declined due to the COVID-19 pandemic, is showing a rapid recovery. Hokkaido and Sapporo, in particular, remain immensely popular for winter sports enthusiasts seeking powder snow and for tourists seeking cool summer temperatures for long-term stays. This recovery in inbound demand is bringing significant benefits to the real estate market in central Sapporo, especially for hotels and commercial facilities.

Against the backdrop of this demand recovery, hotel development is once again becoming active. In addition to the luxury hotel planned as part of the Sapporo Kokusai Building redevelopment, renowned domestic and international hotel brands are planning or considering entry into central Sapporo. The opening of new hotels not only increases accommodation capacity but also creates new jobs and leads to a concentration of high-quality services and restaurants, enhancing the city's international appeal.

This trend has a positive impact not only on investment opportunities in hotels themselves but also on surrounding real estate. It increases the demand for rental housing for hotel employees and for retail and service businesses catering to tourists. Moreover, the increased vibrancy and improved cityscape can be expected to boost the asset value of nearby condominiums. One effective strategy for investors is to monitor areas surrounding major hotel development projects and seek properties that can benefit from the ripple effects of inbound demand.

## 7. Risks for Investors: Demographic Trends and Oversupply Concerns

While the future of central Sapporo is filled with positive indicators, it is crucial for investors to calmly assess the risks. Two points that require particular attention are "macro-level demographic trends" and "localized oversupply."

First, the fact that Japan as a whole has entered an era of population decline is a factor that cannot be ignored in long-term real estate investment. Although Sapporo is one of the few major cities still experiencing population growth, it is projected to shift to a decline in the future. While the trend of people moving back to city centers is expected to keep demand in central areas strong for the time being, long-term trends in actual demand need to be carefully monitored.

Next is the risk of oversupply associated with redevelopment. Currently, there are a plethora of construction plans for office buildings and tower condominiums in central Sapporo. If all these properties are supplied to the market at once, the supply-demand balance could be temporarily disrupted, potentially leading to a drop in rents and a rise in vacancy rates. The office market, in particular, also needs to account for shifts in demand due to changes in work styles (such as the spread of remote work).

Furthermore, one must not forget natural disaster risks. According to the hazard information from "Mekiki Research," the Sapporo Odori Park area has a low risk of landslides (`hasRisk: false`) but there is a risk of flooding (`hasRisk: true`). What is particularly noteworthy is that the maximum potential flood depth is rated as Rank 4, "5 to 10 meters," which is an extremely high level. This indicates that in the event the nearby Toyohira River or other waterways were to overflow, there is a possibility of flooding above the second floor of buildings. When considering a property, it is crucial to check the detailed inundation forecast areas on a hazard map, and to verify factors like the presence of basements, the elevation of electrical equipment, and evacuation routes beforehand. It is necessary to take these risks into account and implement measures such as obtaining appropriate insurance or choosing a unit on a higher floor.
