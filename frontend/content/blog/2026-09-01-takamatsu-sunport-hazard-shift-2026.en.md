## 2. The Full Picture of Sunport Takamatsu Redevelopment and Coastal Storm Surge/Tsunami Risks

The Sunport Takamatsu area, located north of JR Takamatsu Station (JR高松駅) at the gateway to the city, is currently undergoing a massive redevelopment project on a scale rarely seen in recent years. As of 2026, the full scope of this transformation is becoming clear.

[Sunport Takamatsu Redevelopment Timeline (From Groundbreaking to Scheduled Completion)]
*   2021–2023: Ground preparation and infrastructure restructuring for the new station building development.
*   2024: Opening of TAKAMATSU ORNE (タカマツ オルネ), a commercial complex directly connected to JR Takamatsu Station.
*   2025: Completion and opening of the New Kagawa Prefectural Arena / Anabuki Arena Kagawa (新香川県立アリーナ / あなぶきアリーナ香川), one of the largest arenas in the Chugoku and Shikoku regions (中四国).
*   2025–2026: Final stages of construction for foreign luxury hotels (such as Mandarin Oriental (マンダリンオリエンタル)), solidifying the area's role as an international tourism and MICE hub.

As you can see, Sunport Takamatsu holds overwhelming potential to drive business and tourism in Shikoku. However, when evaluating long-term real estate value, "storm surge and tsunami risks" are an unavoidable reality.

The coastal areas of Takamatsu, including Sunport, sit at a low elevation and have a history of storm surge damage from typhoons. Furthermore, in the event of a Nankai Trough Megaquake, tsunamis are projected to travel through the Seto Inland Sea and strike the area. While the redevelopment is bringing glamorous new facilities, investors and developers are conducting strict risk assessments, treating these assets as "properties within projected inundation zones."

Coastal real estate inherently carries a "high risk, high return" profile—offering high profitability in normal times but catastrophic risks during emergencies. This requires investment decisions that go far beyond simple yield calculations.

## 3. Latest Infrastructure Upgrades and BCP Measures for Corporate Attraction in Coastal Areas

Given the acknowledged risks of the coastal areas, why are such massive investments pouring into Sunport Takamatsu? The answer lies in robust Business Continuity Plan (BCP) measures and infrastructure resilience spearheaded by public-private partnerships.

New development projects like the New Kagawa Prefectural Arena and foreign luxury hotels incorporate exceptionally high levels of disaster prevention features right from the design phase. For instance, it has become standard practice to place critical electrical equipment and emergency generators on upper floors, ensuring that even if the ground floor is flooded by a tsunami or storm surge, the facility remains operational. Urban planning also includes seawall reinforcements and the integration of temporary evacuation functions, such as securing vertical evacuation routes via pedestrian decks.

It is precisely because of these thorough infrastructure upgrades that companies can confidently establish operations on the coast. Takamatsu serves as the nerve center for branch office economies in Shikoku. Increasingly, companies are choosing to lease space in high-grade, BCP-equipped office buildings in the Sunport area rather than owning their own standalone buildings.

In other words, we have entered an era where the value of coastal real estate is rated not just by the "presence of hazards," but by the "physical and systemic capacity of the building to withstand those hazards."

> 💡 Analyst's Perspective
> When considering investments in corporate office buildings or commercial facilities, the physical disaster-prevention specs of the property directly impact tenant attraction. A detailed inquiry into the building's disaster prevention equipment is essential.

## 6. Progress Toward a Compact City Centered Around the Kotoden Railway Lines

Takamatsu City is actively promoting the formation of a "Multi-Polar Network-Type Compact City." This strategy aims to curb urban sprawl by concentrating city functions in the center and along public transportation routes. The backbone of this network is the rail infrastructure, including JR and the local beloved Kotoden / Takamatsu-Kotohira Electric Railroad (ことでん / 高松琴平電気鉄道).

The environmental data we pulled includes attractive infrastructure information for an area just outside the city center:

*   Nearest Station: Ritsurin-Koen-Kitaguchi Station on the JR Kotoku Line (JR高徳線「栗林公園北口」駅) (Average daily ridership: 934)
*   School District: Kameoka Elementary School (亀阜小学校), Shiun Junior High School (紫雲中学校)
*   Medical Facilities: 21 locations (including Takamatsu Red Cross Hospital (高松赤十字病院), Gobancho Clinic (五番丁医院), Manabe Clinic (医療法人社団真鍋医院), etc.)
*   Zoning: Category I Mid/High-Rise Exclusive Residential District (Building Coverage Ratio 60%, Floor Area Ratio 200%)

The area around Ritsurin-Koen-Kitaguchi Station is adjacent to Ritsurin Garden, a Special Place of Scenic Beauty, blending natural environments with urban convenience. The Kameoka Elementary and Shiun Junior High school districts, in particular, are known as some of the most highly sought-after educational zones in Takamatsu, boasting rock-solid demand from families with children.
Moreover, the dense concentration of 21 clinics and hospitals in the vicinity—anchored by a highly advanced medical institution like the Takamatsu Red Cross Hospital—serves as a massively powerful draw (and value-retention factor) for the area in an aging society.

While demand is shifting toward the suburbs (Kagawa-cho direction), the asset value of central and near-central areas equipped with such robust medical, educational, and transportation infrastructure will remain unshaken, buoyed by the tailwinds of the city's compact city policies.
