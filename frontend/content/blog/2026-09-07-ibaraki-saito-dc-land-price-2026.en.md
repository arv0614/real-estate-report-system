## 2. Why Northern Ibaraki City is Chosen: Solid Ground and Power Infrastructure Essential for DCs

In selecting a location for data centers, the most critical factors are "resilience against natural disasters" and a "high-capacity, stable power supply network." The primary reason the Saito area in northern Ibaraki City is chosen is that it clears these stringent conditions at a very high level.

Being a hilly area situated at a high elevation and maintaining a certain distance from active faults allows it to physically avoid flood risks—an absolute requirement for hyperscale DCs that must operate 24/7/365 without interruption.

On the other hand, as a real estate investor, you must be cautious about the hazard risks in the "downtown areas" of Ibaraki City. According to actual data retrieved from the system near the center of Ibaraki City, while the risk of sediment-related disasters is evaluated as "No Risk" (phenomenon field blank), the flood risk is assessed as "Present," with a maximum inundation depth rank of "3 (3 to 5 meters)."

An inundation depth of 3 to 5 meters is extremely dangerous, with water reaching from just below the eaves of the second floor to the floor level of the third floor in a typical building. Just because the northern hills are highly resistant to floods does not mean you should blindly assume the entire city is safe. When purchasing real estate such as apartments, condominiums, or commercial buildings in the downtown area, it is vital to verify this flood risk. Strict pre-purchase risk hedging, such as placing electrical equipment on higher floors and selectively investing in safe zones designated on hazard maps, is indispensable.

## 5. Ripple Effects on Surrounding Residential Land and Condo Markets from Job Creation and Infrastructure Growth

While data centers themselves require relatively few on-site staff once operational, the enormous demand during the construction phase and the subsequent influx of related industries—such as IT vendors, maintenance companies, and security firms—create significant local employment. This has resulted in a strong positive impact on Ibaraki City's practical housing and rental condominium markets.

Looking at actual data, there is a transaction case in the Shuharacho (主原町) area where a 135 sqm residential property (land and wooden building, built in 2021) was sold for approximately 50 million yen (Category 1 Residential Zone, 60% coverage, 200% FAR). In the Oda (太田) area, multiple transactions for residential properties (both land only, and land with buildings) ranging from 85 sqm to 140 sqm were completed between 17 million and 25 million yen (some cases hitting a unit price of around 180,000 JPY/sqm). This clearly indicates robust demand from families seeking a quality living environment within a Category 1 Mid/High-Rise Exclusive Residential Zone.

Furthermore, Ibaraki City boasts highly developed lifestyle infrastructure to support its residential environment. Educationally, public school districts like Hozumi Elementary School (穂積小学校) and Seiryo Junior High School (西陵中学校) are well-established, encouraging the influx of child-rearing households. Most notably, the medical environment is exceptionally rich; according to our research data, there are as many as "56 medical facilities" around the target area. A diverse array of medical infrastructure is densely packed, ranging from comprehensive hospitals like Tanigawa Memorial Hospital (Medical Corporation Tokuseikai) (谷川記念病院（医療法人篤靜会）) and Nisshokai Hospital (Medical Corporation Nisshokai) (日翔会病院（医療法人社団日翔会）) to community-based clinics like Ibaraki Clinic (Public Interest Incorporated Foundation Yodogawa Workers Welfare Association) (茨木診療所（公益財団法人 淀川勤労者厚生協会附属）).

## 6. Urban Planning in the Saito Area and the Latest Trends in Eco-Friendly Data Center Development (2026)

An unavoidable theme in real estate development in 2026 is "Environment (ESG)." Particularly in data center development, which consumes vast amounts of electricity and generates significant waste heat, harmonizing with the surrounding environment and utilizing renewable energy have become major challenges in urban planning.

Ibaraki City's urban planning employs flexible and strategic zoning to accommodate these next-generation industries. According to the acquired environmental data, specific "Neighborhood Commercial Zones" within Ibaraki City permit highly intensive land use, with a building coverage ratio of "80%" and a floor area ratio (FAR) of "400%." This allows for highly efficient facility operations through high-rise and high-density construction even on limited plot sizes.

At the same time, developments in hilly terrains like the Saito area are subject to strict eco-friendly mandates. Regulations ensuring green space ratios and design guidelines based on landscape ordinances are enforced to prevent massive, sterile infrastructure facilities from destroying the surrounding natural and residential environments. From an investor's perspective, real estate that considers these ESG factors (such as Green Buildings) is a highly promising investment target, as it garners higher valuations (Green Premiums) from institutional investors during future tenant leasing or property exits.
