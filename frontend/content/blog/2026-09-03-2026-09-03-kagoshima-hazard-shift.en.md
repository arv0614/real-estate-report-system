## 2. Current Status of Complex Disaster Risks in Kagoshima City (Tsunamis, Storm Surges, Landslides, and Sakurajima Volcanic Ash)

In real estate investment and home purchasing, hazard information is now the most critical factor to consider. The data obtained this time confirmed that extremely serious risks are inherent in certain areas of Kagoshima City.

- Flood Risk: Present (Max Inundation Depth Rank 4: 5 to 10 meters)
- Landslide Risk: Present (Phenomenon: Debris Flow)

※WARNING※
A maximum inundation depth of "5 to 10 meters" is an extremely dangerous level, suggesting the possibility of buildings being submerged up to their second or third floors. Furthermore, if a "debris flow" occurs, it causes fatal damage, leading to building collapse and loss of life. When considering properties in Kagoshima City, do not rely solely on yield and price; be sure to cross-reference with the hazard maps of each municipality before purchasing to strictly verify whether the target property falls within these risk areas.

The complex disaster risk, combining the fragile geology characteristic of the Shirasu Plateau with storm surges and torrential rains during typhoons, has begun to function as an undeniable negative factor (risk premium) in Kagoshima City's real estate valuation.

## 3. Changes in Real Estate Transactions and Stricter Risk Assessments in Coastal and Low-Lying Areas

The area around Tenmonkan (天文館), Kagoshima City's commercial center, and coastal low-lying areas boast high convenience and a concentration of economic activities. According to actual data, the nearest station, Tenmonkandori Station (天文館通駅) (operated by Kagoshima City), sees 4,578 daily passengers, and the surrounding area is designated as a "Commercial Zone" with an 80% Building Coverage Ratio (BCR) and 400% Floor Area Ratio (FAR).

Additionally, the school districts include Meizan Elementary School (名山小学校) and Nagata Junior High School (長田中学校), and the area houses a staggering 101 medical institutions (such as Takada Hospital (高田病院), Izuro Imamura Hospital (いづろ今村病院), and Sakimoto Hospital (﨑元病院)). This overwhelming concentration of medical infrastructure is a significant advantage, particularly for the residential needs of seniors and investments in commercial real estate like clinic buildings.

However, as mentioned earlier, much of this area has a low elevation above sea level, carrying a flood risk of "5 to 10 meters" during storm surges and river floods. Consequently, in recent years, a "strictification of risk assessment" has been progressing on the ground level. Properties without flood countermeasures—such as using the first floor for pilotis (parking) and placing residential and electrical equipment on the second floor or higher—face stricter loan screenings or struggle to attract tenants.

## 5. Latest Trends in Area-Specific Land Price Structures Brought About by Disaster Hazard Map Revisions

Urban land price structures are constantly updated by changes in awareness of hazard risks and the subsequent progress of redevelopment. By organizing the timeline of major recent redevelopments in Kagoshima City, it becomes clear that disaster prevention and the reorganization of urban functions are closely linked.

| Period | Trends in Major Redevelopment and Infrastructure Development |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2021 - | Progress in the development of the Kagoshima-Chuo Station (鹿児島中央駅) West Exit area (mixed-use commercial, office, and residential). |

| 2025 - 2026 | Full-scale implementation of the redevelopment plan for the Kagoshima Port Honko Area (鹿児島港本港区). Creation of waterfront spaces and enhancement of storm surge/tsunami countermeasures. |

While redevelopment investments in the central urban area (around Tenmonkan and Kagoshima-Chuo Station) remain active, coastal areas like the "Kagoshima Port Honko Area" require developments that incorporate protective functions against storm surges and tsunamis (a super-levee approach) rather than simple commercial development.

Conversely, although elevated areas have established popularity as residential zones, they face challenges such as traffic congestion on access roads crawling up steep slopes and cliff areas with debris flow risks (increasing maintenance costs for retaining walls). Future land price trends will become more strictly selective, shifting from the phase of "any elevated land is good" to "flat land within elevated areas where infrastructure is updated and completely separated from landslide risks."

## 6. Trends in Newly Built Condominiums and Detached Houses with Enhanced Disaster Resilience

In response to these changes in the land price structure, the supply strategies of developers and house builders have also shifted significantly.

For newly built condominiums supplied in low-lying and coastal commercial zones (e.g., FAR 400%), designs premised on inundation risks (5 to 10 meters) have become the standard. Specifically, power receiving equipment (cubicles) and emergency generators are installed on rooftops or middle floors, and waterproof barriers are fully equipped at first-floor entrances. Furthermore, an increasing number of properties feature unique ventilation systems considering Sakurajima's (桜島) volcanic ash and the adoption of enclosed inner balconies. These "disaster prevention premiums" push up construction costs, resulting in an upward trend in the sales prices of new condominiums.

Meanwhile, the supply of detached houses dominates in inland areas like Ishidani-cho and elevated areas like Ishikidai. Here, due to caution against landslide risks (debris flows), proving the safety of retaining walls behind properties and thorough ground improvement work are required. As seen in the aforementioned transaction of a 1,300 sqm steel-framed factory in Ishidani-cho (approx. 48 million yen), an increasing number of companies are relocating their own buildings and factories to inland semi-industrial zones from a BCP (Business Continuity Planning) perspective. Investments in "safe ground" are accelerating on both the residential and industrial fronts.
