Matsue, Shimane Prefecture, is known as the "City of Water," home to the national treasure Matsue Castle and the stunning scenery of Lake Shinji (Shinjiko). As the core city of the San'in region, it has cultivated a unique history and culture. However, like many other regional cities in Japan, it faces the structural challenges of population decline and an aging society. The key to overcoming this situation and paving the way for a sustainable future is the "Matsue Station Area Redevelopment Plan," which is currently in its planning stages.

This plan is more than just a renovation of the station building or a beautification of the station front. It is based on a grand vision for 2030 to fundamentally strengthen the station's function as a transportation hub and concentrate commercial and business functions, thereby creating a new gateway for Matsue and the entire San'in region. When the flow of people changes and the urban framework is reorganized, the real estate market is the first to react dynamically.

In this article, from the perspective of a veteran real estate analyst, we will thoroughly analyze the impact of this Matsue Station area redevelopment on the real estate market. Using actual transaction data and environmental data obtained by "Mekiki Research" as evidence, we will take a multifaceted look at Matsue's real estate potential leading up to 2030, key points for investors to watch, and potential risks.

## 1. Introduction: The Current State of Matsue, a San'in Powerhouse, and Its Real Estate Market Challenges

Matsue is an international city of culture and tourism that attracts many visitors throughout the year. It also serves as the administrative and economic center of Shimane Prefecture as its capital. However, its real estate market is not immune to the major national trend of population concentration in major urban centers and outflow from regional areas.

First, let's grasp the overall picture of Matsue's real estate market using concrete data. According to 3,040 real estate transaction records collected by "Mekiki Research" from 2021 to 2025, the average transaction price for the entire city of Matsue is approximately ¥16.4 million. However, the median price, which is considered to be a more accurate representation, is ¥9.8 million, revealing a gap of about ¥6.6 million. This suggests that a few high-value properties, such as the highest transaction of ¥1.2 billion included in the data, are pulling up the average, and that most transactions are centered around the ¥10 million mark.

The average price per square meter, which indicates land value, is approximately ¥43,000/m², a standard level for a regional core city. However, this is a city-wide average, and there is a significant price difference between the central urban area and the suburbs. For example, transaction samples show land in "Kashima-cho Takeyo," which is outside the city planning area, trading at ¥9,500/m², while land in "Kasuga-cho," closer to the city center, is priced at ¥20,000/m², more than double the price.

Under these circumstances, the challenges Matsue faces are the "diffusion of urban functions" and a "decline in the vitality of the central urban area." In a society with a declining population, the cost of maintaining dispersed urban functions increases and becomes inefficient. The station area redevelopment is a clear solution to this problem, a crucial strategy to promote a "compact city" by concentrating urban functions in the central area and thereby maintaining and enhancing real estate values.

## 2. The Core of the Matsue Station Area Redevelopment Plan and its Roadmap to 2030

The "Matsue Station Area Redevelopment Plan" currently under consideration aims to elevate Matsue Station into a new hub for the San'in region, with 2030 as a target year. Its core can be summarized in the following three pillars:

1.  Maximizing its function as a transportation hub: Currently, platforms for trains, buses, and taxis at Matsue Station are dispersed, posing a challenge to the convenience of transfers. The plan aims to consolidate these transportation modes into a plaza directly connected to the station, enabling seamless transfers. This will dramatically improve convenience not only for daily use by residents but also for tourists and business travelers from a wider area.
2.  Concentrating commercial, business, and community functions: The plan involves attracting new commercial facilities, high-spec offices, and community facilities with convention capabilities to the station building and surrounding areas. This will transform the station area from a mere transit point into a destination where people stay, work, and interact. In particular, attracting appealing tenants that draw in young people and the business community will be key to revitalizing the entire area.
3.  Integrating the north and south sides of the station and strengthening the urban axis: The plan will promote integrated urban development by connecting the north and south sides of the station, currently divided by the railway, with new pedestrian walkways and decks. This will facilitate the flow of people and unlock the development potential of the station's south side. By merging the historic landscape of the north exit, which leads to Matsue Castle, with the new urban functions of the south exit, the entire urban axis of Matsue will be strengthened.

This plan is not something that will be completed overnight; it is a long-term project that will be implemented in stages leading up to 2030. However, as the plan becomes more concrete, the real estate market will begin to price in expectations. For investors, it will be crucial to carefully monitor the progress of this plan and enter the market at the right time.

## 3. The Impact of a Stronger Transportation Hub: Improved Accessibility from the Wider Region

The enhancement of transportation infrastructure is one of the most critical factors influencing real estate value. Matsue Station is a major station in the San'in region, served by the San'in Main Line and the Kisuki Line, and its importance is reflected in the data. According to "Mekiki Research," the average daily number of passengers at Matsue Station is 8,730, and it is already used by many people as a key base for the West Japan Railway Company (JR West) in the San'in region.

Strengthening the station's function as a transportation hub through redevelopment will make this flow of people even larger and more diverse. For example, if limousine buses from Izumo Enmusubi Airport and Yonago Kitaro Airport, as well as highway buses connecting to various parts of the prefecture and neighboring prefectures, are functionally arranged in the station front plaza for smooth transfers with the railway, Matsue will solidify its position as the "gateway to the San'in region."

This improvement in accessibility will have two major impacts on the real estate market.
First, it will stimulate business demand. Easier access from a wider area will increase demand from companies looking to establish branches or sales offices around Matsue Station. This will directly lead to higher office building rents and lower vacancy rates, increasing the appeal of office real estate investment.
Second, it will expand tourism demand. FITs (Free and Independent Travelers) and inbound tourists will be more likely to choose Matsue Station as their base for touring the San'in region using public transportation. This will increase demand for hotels and commercial facilities around the station, pushing up the value of these properties. Improved transportation convenience will be a powerful driver for creating real demand in real estate.

## 4. How Will the Concentration of Commercial and Office Functions Change Land Prices? Forecasting Official Land Price Trends

When commercial and office functions are concentrated through redevelopment, the profitability of land in that area increases dramatically, becoming a direct factor in land price appreciation. The area around Matsue Station already has high potential. According to environmental data from "Mekiki Research," the area around the station is designated as a "Commercial Zone," with a building coverage ratio (which regulates building density) of 80% and a floor-area ratio (which determines the maximum total floor space) of 400%. This means that high-rise, high-density land use is legally permitted, indicating that conditions are ripe for large-scale redevelopment.

Currently, commercial land prices in Matsue have remained relatively stable amidst a nationwide downward trend, but redevelopment could be the catalyst to shift this trend into a clear upward trajectory. The opening of new commercial facilities would raise the rental market rates for nearby street-level shops, which in turn would push up the income-based value of the land. Similarly, if a state-of-the-art office building is constructed and attracts prominent companies, the value of existing office buildings in the vicinity will also be reassessed.

This land price increase is predicted to ripple outwards in concentric circles from the station. First, land prices within the redevelopment area will rise sharply, followed by convenient areas within a 5- to 10-minute walk from the station. In this process, landowners of existing old buildings will have opportunities for capital gains through rebuilding or selling. For investors, a strategy of making early investments in surrounding areas before the ripple effects of redevelopment take hold could be effective. The trend in official land prices (Koji-chika) will be the most important indicator for measuring the effect of this redevelopment.

## 5. Housing Market Outlook Amid Population Decline: The Compact City Concept and Demand for Condominiums

In Matsue, where the population continues to decline, the housing market may at first glance seem to be in a tough situation. However, the "compact city" concept, linked with redevelopment, will bring new demand and structural changes to the housing market. The idea of a compact city—guiding the population to the convenient city center and concentrating urban functions like medical care, welfare, and commerce within walking distance—is strongly supported, especially by the elderly who may find it difficult to drive, as well as by younger and single-person households who seek a close proximity between home and workplace.

As a result, a "return to the city center" movement towards the Matsue Station area is expected to accelerate, and housing demand is predicted to shift from suburban single-family homes to condominiums near the station. In particular, condominiums, which require less maintenance and offer high security, are a housing type that matches diverse modern lifestyles.

## 6. National Treasure Matsue Castle and Inbound Demand: The Impact of Tourism DX on Real Estate Value

Another important pillar supporting Matsue's economy is its rich tourism resources, such as the national treasure Matsue Castle and Lake Shinji. The station area redevelopment will play a crucial role in maximizing the value of these resources and linking tourism demand, including from inbound visitors, to real estate value.

The new Matsue Station will become the "starting point of the journey" for tourists. Sophisticated tourist information centers, baggage storage services, and multilingual digital signage will significantly improve tourist satisfaction. Furthermore, by smoothly coordinating secondary transportation (such as loop buses and bike-sharing services) from the station to major tourist spots like Matsue Castle and the Shiomi Nawate district, city-wide sightseeing will be promoted, leading to longer stays and increased spending by tourists.

The new station will also function as a hub for promoting "Tourism DX (Digital Transformation)," which has been gaining attention in recent years. For example, this could include providing historical experience content using VR/AR technology or offering personalized tourist information through mobile apps. Such new tourism experiences will enhance Matsue's brand value and act as a powerful magnet to attract repeat visitors and inbound tourists.

This expansion of tourism demand will bring direct benefits to the real estate market around the station, especially hotels and commercial facilities. Demand for land for new hotel development and for restaurant and retail spaces targeting inbound tourists will increase, pushing up the rents and transaction prices for these properties. Intangible assets like tourism will be converted into the tangible asset value of real estate, with redevelopment acting as the catalyst.

## 7. Analysis of Key Areas and Risk Factors for Investors

Based on the analysis so far, the primary area for investors to watch is the north and south sides of Matsue Station, where the redevelopment will take place directly. Here, opportunities include participating in large-scale development projects for commercial buildings, offices, and condominiums, or acquiring properties after completion. The next area of interest is within a 10-minute walk from the station, where ripple effects from the redevelopment are expected. In this area, existing used condominiums, older buildings with redevelopment potential, and small plots of land could be attractive investment targets.

However, all investments come with risks. When considering real estate around Matsue Station, one risk that cannot be ignored is natural disasters. According to hazard information from "Mekiki Research," this area faces a flood inundation risk, with a maximum expected flood depth of "3 to 5 meters" (Rank 3), a relatively high level. This can be considered a geographical inevitability for central Matsue, which is close to Lake Shinji and the Ohashi River. Therefore, when acquiring a property, it is essential to check the detailed hazard map published by the local government to confirm the inundation risk of the site and to scrutinize whether measures such as raising the building's foundation or installing flood barriers have been taken, and whether appropriate fire and flood insurance is in place. Fortunately, no landslide risk has been indicated, but flood countermeasures are an essential consideration.

Another point to note is the comprehensiveness of available data. This survey confirmed 18 medical institutions around Matsue Station, indicating a degree of living convenience. On the other hand, public elementary and junior high school district information (`schools`) was not available (null). This means that when considering housing for families, it is necessary to separately confirm accurate information on the city hall's website or at their service counter.

Attention must also be paid to the liquidity of the real estate market and the specificity of individual property prices. While the total number of transaction samples for Matsue City is ample at 3,040, the data becomes limited when narrowed down to specific areas or property types. Individual transactions are influenced by various factors such as the condition of the building, road access, and the seller's circumstances. Therefore, the key to success is to not rely solely on average data but to carefully conduct individual property evaluations.
