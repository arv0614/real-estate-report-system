## 1. Just Before Disaster Prevention Day: The Importance of Hazard Maps in Miyagino Ward, Sendai City

Since the prior explanation of a property's location on flood hazard maps became mandatory under the 2020 revision of the Building Lots and Buildings Transaction Business Act (宅地建物取引業法), hazard maps have established themselves as one of the most critical documents in real estate transactions. As of 2026, caution toward "color-coded (risk-designated) areas on the map" has grown immensely not only among institutional investors but also among general homebuyers, becoming a decisive factor that dictates asset value.

In this context, examining the latest hazard data for a specific inland location in Miyagino Ward, Sendai City (仙台市宮城野区) (latitude 38.268889, longitude 140.902222), obtained from the backend system of Mekiki Research (物件目利きリサーチ) (utilizing the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (国土交通省) API and Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (国土地理院) hazard data), yields highly suggestive results. This area is designated as "no phenomenon (no risk)" for landslide disasters, sparing it from the collapse risks typical of hilly or cliff areas. On the other hand, it is flagged as "at risk" for flooding, indicating a maximum inundation depth of "5 to 10 meters (Rank 4)"—a highly alarming figure.

An inundation depth of "5 to 10 meters" means a severe flood risk where a standard two-story wooden house would be completely submerged up to the roof, and muddy water would reach just below the floor of the third story in an apartment building. Although this area is physically distant from the Pacific coast and often perceived as having a low direct tsunami risk, it harbors latent risks of flooding from small and medium-sized rivers like the Umeda River (梅田川) and inland flooding caused by record-breaking heavy rains.

When considering a property purchase, we strongly warn our readers not to be complacent just because it is an inland area safe from tsunamis. Instead, this localized flood risk of "5 to 10 meters" must be thoroughly understood and factored into countermeasures before buying. Carefully decoding hazard maps to correctly recognize flood risks is the very first step in real estate investment.

## 2. 15 Years Since the Great East Japan Earthquake: Lessons Learned and the History of Area Reorganization

Fifteen years have passed since the Great East Japan Earthquake (東日本大震災) on March 11, 2011, and Sendai City’s urban structure and demographics have undergone massive transformations. As mentioned earlier, Miyagino Ward encompasses diverse terrains—from the coastal Sendai Port (仙台港), Nakano (中野), and Gamo (蒲生) areas to the inland Haramachi (原町), Tsubamesawa (燕沢), and Tsutsujigaoka (榴ケ岡) areas—creating a stark contrast in the tsunami damage sustained across the ward.

Immediately following the earthquake, the government vigorously promoted the Disaster Relief Group Relocation Promotion Project (防災集団移転促進事業) and Land Readjustment Project (土地区画整理事業). This sparked a large-scale population shift from the severely tsunami-inundated eastern coastal regions to elevated inland areas with solid ground and no tsunami reach risk. This drastic "area reorganization" went beyond merely relocating disaster victims; it became a historical turning point that determined the subsequent development of road networks, commercial facility planning, and the direction of real estate development by private developers.

Now, 15 years later in 2026, the disaster-stricken areas have fully transitioned from the "recovery phase" centered on infrastructure restoration to a "sustainable urban management phase" aiming for economic independence. However, while society's collective memory may gradually fade, the "pricing of disaster risks" in the real estate market has become even more severe and precise than it was back then. Financial institutions' lending criteria and non-life insurance companies' underwriting standards are tightening year by year, and past lessons have been systematized as data, taking deep root in the foundation of modern real estate evaluation.

## 3. Current State of Coastal Risk Areas (Nakano and Gamo Areas) and Real Estate Evaluation

Before the earthquake, the coastal areas representing the eastern part of Miyagino Ward, such as the Nakano and Gamo districts, were characterized by idyllic detached residential neighborhoods and rich agricultural land. Today, however, due to Sendai City designating them as disaster hazard zones and implementing strict land-use conversion policies, developing new residential areas is extremely limited or practically impossible.

On the flip side, maximizing the geographical advantage of hosting Sendai Port—the largest logistics hub in the Tohoku region—these areas have seen a rapid surge in land utilization for "industrial real estate." This includes large, advanced logistics facilities (multi-tenant logistics centers), manufacturing plants, and industrial mega-solar power plants utilizing vast idle lands. Driven by the expansion of the e-commerce market, the demand for logistics is robust, and the need for land as industrial hubs maintains a certain level.

Nevertheless, land values for residential or general commercial use fall far short of pre-disaster levels, and the price gap with inland areas continues to widen. When evaluating and acquiring coastal properties for investment (e.g., warehouses with business fixed-term land leases), robust BCP (Business Continuity Planning) measures against potential tsunamis and storm surges from super typhoons are mandatory. Even with advanced seawall construction, substantial initial investments and maintenance costs are required for measures like installing emergency power generators at high elevations and deploying waterproof gates. Consequently, backed by soaring insurance premiums and strict collateral valuations by financial institutions (such as lower LTV ratios), a notable trend in 2026 is that investors are demanding higher risk premiums (higher cap rates/expected yields).

## 4. Soaring Land Prices and Re-evaluation of Elevated Inland Areas (Haramachi and Tsubamesawa Areas)

In stark contrast to the coastal regions, the inland areas of Miyagino Ward that escaped tsunami risks (such as Haramachi, Tsubamesawa, Odawara (小田原), and Gorin (五輪)) have been re-evaluated as "safe and highly convenient residential areas" right from the immediate aftermath of the earthquake. These areas have continued to maintain soaring land prices and steady end-user demand.

The closest station to the central point we obtained actual data for is JR Senseki Line (JR仙石線) Rikuzen-Haranomachi Station (陸前原ノ町駅), operated by East Japan Railway Company (東日本旅客鉄道). With 6,708 passengers boarding and alighting daily, it is a vibrant hub for commuting to work and school. Checking the environmental data around this station reveals that the land-use zone under the City Planning Act is designated as a Neighborhood Commercial Zone (近隣商業地域), allowing for extremely high-density land use with a building coverage ratio of 80% and a floor area ratio of 300%. Thanks to this relaxed 300% floor area ratio, private developers have actively constructed mid-to-high-rise condominiums and rental residences, significantly transforming the local skyline.

Furthermore, the richness of the surrounding environment as a residential area is noteworthy. It falls within the school districts of the prestigious Haramachi Elementary School (原町小学校) and Miyagino Junior High School (宮城野中学校), garnering enduring popularity among child-rearing families. As for medical institutions—the cornerstone of living infrastructure—a total of 14 facilities are densely packed within the area. Specifically, the area boasts Nakajima Hospital (中嶋病院) acting as a regional core, along with primary care clinics like Haramachi Sasaki Clinic (原町ささき内科) and Tanno Dermatology, Internal Medicine and Pediatrics Clinic (丹野皮膚科内科小児科). The data clearly shows that this excellent medical access firmly underpins the real estate value of the inland areas.

## 6. Impact on Rental Demand: Area Selection Criteria for Families and Singles

The clear polarization of land prices is directly linked to demand trends and rent settings in the rental market. Analyzing the rental demand in Miyagino Ward as of 2026 by persona reveals distinct differences in area selection criteria and hazard map perception between families and singles.

Families, particularly those with dual incomes, tend to prioritize educational and medical environments above all else. Being situated within good school districts like the aforementioned Haramachi Elementary School and Miyagino Junior High School, along with a comprehensive medical network of 14 facilities including pediatric care like Tanno Dermatology, Internal Medicine and Pediatrics Clinic, serves as an extremely powerful selling point for securing tenants.
Furthermore, they carry the memory of the earthquake and are highly sensitive to hazard maps. While choosing an area free of tsunami risk is a given prerequisite, when looking for properties in the "5 to 10 meters flood depth" risk areas mentioned in Chapter 1, they increasingly insist on the absolute condition that "the residential floor must be on the 3rd floor or higher, exceeding the anticipated inundation depth."

On the other hand, singles who balance remote work and office commuting tend to prioritize proximity to stations like Rikuzen-Haranomachi Station and access to commercial facilities around the station. However, in recent years, disaster awareness has improved even among singles, leading to a growing aversion to first-floor units (due to inland flooding risks and security concerns). As a result, even within the same rental condominium, the rent gap between the first floor and the second floor and above (the discount rate for the first floor) has widened compared to before. This necessitates factoring in the vacancy and rent-drop risks of first-floor units more strictly during yield calculations.

The presence or absence of such design philosophies accommodating BCP (Business Continuity Planning) directly affects the increase or decrease of loan valuation amounts by financial institutions and the calculation of soaring water disaster insurance premiums.
Correctly quantifying disaster risks and comprehensively evaluating the building's resilience performance is the true essence of advanced, professional "property evaluation" in 2026.
