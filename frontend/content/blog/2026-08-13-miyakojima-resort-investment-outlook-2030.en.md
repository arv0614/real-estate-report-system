## 1. Why Miyako Island, Why Now? The New Frontier of Resort Real Estate Investment

Surrounded by some of the world's most stunning waters, famously known as "Miyako Blue," Miyako Island (Miyakojima) in Okinawa Prefecture was once a remote getaway known only to a select few. However, its name gained international recognition following the opening of the Irabu Ohashi Bridge in 2015 and the international terminal at Shimojishima Airport in 2019. Now, in the post-COVID era with the full-scale recovery of inbound tourism on the horizon, Miyako Island is once again drawing keen interest as a new frontier for resort real estate investment.

In this article, we at Mekiki Research will conduct a multi-faceted analysis of the potential and challenges of Miyako Island's real estate market, looking ahead to the near future of 2030. Our analysis is based on actual transaction data we have independently acquired. This is not just a tourist guide; we will delve deep from a professional standpoint, using concrete figures to grasp the market's "current position," assess the impact of recovering tourism and new developments on land prices, and explore the strategies investors should consider.

## 3. The Impact of Inbound Tourism Recovery and Airline Route Expansion

The future of Miyako Island's real estate market is inextricably linked to trends in inbound tourism. The international terminal at Shimojishima Airport, in particular, carries high expectations as the island's new air gateway. International flights, which were temporarily suspended during the pandemic, are being progressively resumed and newly launched, with plans for direct routes from major Asian cities like Hong Kong, Seoul, and Taipei.

The expansion of airline routes will have two major impacts on the real estate market:

1.  Increased Tourism-Related Demand:
    A rise in international tourists will directly boost demand for hotels, commercial facilities, and rental car businesses. This is expected to drive up the value of commercial land and retail properties, as well as push up rental rates. Areas with good access from the airport and land fronting beautiful beaches are likely to benefit the most.

2.  Stimulation of Vacation and Second Home Demand:
    Wealthy international visitors captivated by Miyako Island's charm may increasingly seek to purchase vacation homes and second homes. The highest transaction price of ¥1.5 billion seen in the data suggests the potential for large-scale developments by foreign capital or acquisitions of ultra-luxury properties by wealthy individuals. This trend is expected to accelerate as access from the rest of Asia improves.

However, it's worth noting that in the data for the area around Miyako Island City Hall, the nearest station information (`station.name`) is `null`, indicating that island life is completely car-dependent. Therefore, when considering properties for tourists or new residents, key evaluation points include not only the distance from the airport but also the availability of parking and convenient access to major tourist spots and commercial facilities.

## 4. The Forefront of Active Luxury Resort and Villa Development

Miyako Island is seeing a succession of luxury resort hotel and private villa developments by major domestic and international developers. The prime areas for this activity include the islands connected by bridges—Irabu, Kurima, and Ikema—as well as scenic spots along the east coast.

These developments not only directly push up surrounding land prices but also enhance the island's overall brand image. The presence of luxury resorts helps establish Miyako as a "premium travel destination," attracting more high-net-worth visitors.

These development trends can also present significant opportunities for individual investors. For example:

*   Acquiring land near development zones: A strategy of investing early in areas surrounding planned large-scale developments in anticipation of future price appreciation.
*   Developing small-scale villas and condominiums: A strategy to develop and operate smaller, more private accommodations that differentiate from major developers.
*   Renovating existing properties: A strategy of acquiring older properties near development zones, renovating them to resort specifications, and then renting them out or reselling them.

However, caution is necessary. Miyako Island has been strengthening its ordinances to protect its natural environment and landscapes, and development comes with various regulations. It is essential for prospective investors to thoroughly review municipal ordinances and city plans beforehand.

## 5. Shifting Demographics: The Impact of a Growing Migrant Population on the Housing Market

Miyako Island's appeal extends beyond just tourists. Fueled by the spread of remote work and diversifying lifestyles, the number of people relocating from mainland Japan is on the rise. This "demand for permanent and long-term residence" forms another crucial market segment, distinct from resort demand.

What these new residents seek isn't necessarily a luxury property with an ocean view. Instead, they look for reasonably priced homes in areas with well-established living infrastructure, such as supermarkets, schools, and hospitals.

## 6. Investment Caveats: Typhoon Hazards and Sustainability Considerations

When considering real estate investment on Miyako Island, the risk of natural disasters, especially typhoons, is unavoidable. The Okinawa region is frequently in the path of typhoons, and meticulous attention must be paid to a property's structure and location.

The hazard data for the area around Miyako Island City Hall shows that the risks for both "flood" and "landslide" are "hasRisk: false," indicating that this specific point is relatively safe. However, this is pinpoint data and does not guarantee the safety of the entire island. In particular, low-lying coastal land requires a separate assessment for storm surge and tsunami risks during typhoons. When selecting a property, it is essential to thoroughly check the building's structure (reinforced concrete is preferable), wind protection for windows, and the official hazard maps.

Furthermore, in line with recent global trends, sustainability has become a critical criterion in investment decisions. Miyako Island's unique natural environment is both its greatest tourist asset and a resource that must be protected. Environmental degradation from excessive development could diminish the island's charm in the medium to long term, leading to a decline in property values.

In the future, real estate that incorporates sustainability—such as environmentally friendly construction methods, renewable energy, and proper wastewater treatment facilities—will likely be valued more highly. Investors are now expected to adopt a long-term perspective that prioritizes coexistence with Miyako's nature, rather than focusing solely on short-term profits.

## 7. Area-by-Area Analysis: Investment Potential in Hirara, Irabu Island, and Kurima Island

Finally, let's analyze the characteristics and investment potential of some of Miyako Island's most noteworthy areas.

### Hirara Urban Area
Hirara is the administrative and economic heart of Miyako Island, home to the city hall, prefectural government branch offices, commercial facilities, and restaurants. As mentioned, the real data confirms it is a "Category 1 Medium-to-High-Rise Exclusive Residential District" with designated school districts and 16 medical facilities, making it the area with the most developed living infrastructure.
*   Investment Potential: There is stable demand for rental housing from new residents and corporate transferees, making it suitable for long-term income gain. It also has high potential as a commercial district, with promising investment opportunities in retail and office properties.

### Irabu Island (Irabujima)
Since the opening of the Irabu Ohashi Bridge in 2015, this area has rapidly integrated with the main island of Miyako. It is home to Shimojishima Airport and numerous scenic spots like Toguchi-no-hama Beach and Tooriike Pond, making it a hotbed for resort development.
*   Investment Potential: As confirmed by the real data, a diverse range of properties are traded here, from ¥10 million residential plots to ¥800,000 farmland, indicating significant potential for price appreciation. Coastal land with scenic views is attracting strong interest from both domestic and international investors for villa development.

### Kurima Island (Kurimajima)
A small island connected to Miyako by the Kurima Bridge, it has established its own unique brand with stylish cafes and boutiques. The view from the Ryugujo Observatory is spectacular, making it popular with tourists seeking a sophisticated atmosphere.
*   Investment Potential: This area is better suited for smaller, design-oriented projects like boutique hotels, villas, and cafes rather than large-scale developments. A project with a concept that aligns with the island's atmosphere can expect high profitability.
